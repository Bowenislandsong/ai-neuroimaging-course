### F01 · [What the AI does and what you must understand](../../notebooks/00_foundations/01_learning_contract.ipynb)

*Technique:* Input-to-output transformation contracts, axis semantics, and temporal scaling invariants (demeaning, percent signal change, z-scoring)

Every neuroimaging preprocessing and modeling step transforms array shapes, physical units, and statistical conditioning sets. Without an explicit transformation contract, a syntactically valid snippet can demean across space instead of time (passing a naive global-mean check while injecting global-signal artifacts) or alter group-level GLM effect sizes (beta_hat) across coil sensitivity profiles even though single-voxel t-statistics remain invariant.

### F02 · [From tissue to MRI contrast to BOLD](../../notebooks/00_foundations/02_measurement_bridge.ipynb)

*Technique:* Bloch relaxation (T1, T2, T2*), Gradient-Echo BOLD sensitivity, and neurovascular hemodynamic coupling

MRI voxel intensities are sequence-weighted echo amplitudes rather than direct measurements of tissue density or spiking activity. Understanding how TR, TE, and flip angle invert tissue contrast, why BOLD contrast peaks near TE = T2* while suffering susceptibility dropout near air-tissue sinuses, and why BOLD reflects perisynaptic LFP input filtered through a sluggish, region-specific hemodynamic response (Logothetis et al., 2001) prevents reverse-inference and vascular-latency confounds in fMRI.

- **Foundational.** Ogawa S, Lee TM, Kay AR, Tank DW (1990). [Brain magnetic resonance imaging with contrast dependent on blood oxygenation](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.87.24.9868). *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*. Cited by 6,589 (OpenAlex); MATCH.  
  Introduces Blood-Oxygenation-Level-Dependent (BOLD) MRI contrast arising from paramagnetic deoxyhemoglobin field gradients shortening T2*.
- **Foundational.** Buxton RB, Wong EC, Frank LR (1998). [Dynamics of blood flow and oxygenation changes during brain activation: The balloon model](https://doi.org/10.1002/mrm.1910390602). *Magnetic Resonance in Medicine*. Cited by 1,726 (OpenAlex); MATCH.  
  Formulates the biomechanical Balloon model linking cerebral blood flow surges, venous volume expansion, and deoxyhemoglobin clearance to the delayed BOLD impulse response.
- **Foundational.** Logothetis NK, Pauls J, Augath M, Trinath T, Oeltermann A (2001). [Neurophysiological investigation of the basis of the fMRI signal](https://doi.org/10.1038/35084005). *Nature*. Cited by 6,524 (OpenAlex); MATCH. Course library: [PB01](measurement.md#pb01).  
  Simultaneous intracortical electrophysiology and 4.7 T fMRI proving that BOLD correlates more strongly with perisynaptic local field potentials (LFPs) than with multi-unit spiking output (MUA).
- **Review.** Drew PJ (2019). [Vascular and neural basis of the BOLD signal](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.conb.2019.06.004). *Current Opinion in Neurobiology*. Cited by 150 (OpenAlex); MATCH.  
  Synthesizes modern cellular and vascular mechanisms of neurovascular coupling, highlighting how interneurons, non-neural drives, and regional vascular dynamics shape BOLD signals.

### F03 · [Read a snippet without becoming a software engineer](../../notebooks/00_foundations/03_snippet_literacy.ipynb)

*Technique:* Forensic Python/NumPy code auditing: broadcasting semantics, domain guards, view vs. copy aliasing, mask indexing, and fold-isolated feature selection

Auditing code line-by-line as a mathematical contract prevents fabricated decoding accuracy and corrupted baselines.

### F04 · [A notebook is an experiment with state](../../notebooks/00_foundations/04_notebook_environment.ipynb)

*Technique:* Jupyter kernel state hygiene, PRNG stream isolation (SeedSequence), IEEE-754 floating-point determinism, and cryptographic provenance

[Reproducibility of neuroimaging analyses across operating systems](https://doi.org/10.3389/fninf.2015.00012). *Frontiers in Neuroinformatics*. Cited by 154 (OpenAlex); MATCH.

### PR01 · [Read an image without losing its location](../../notebooks/01_processing/01_images_coordinates_metadata.ipynb)

*Technique:* Voxel-to-world affine geometry, NIfTI qform/sform headers, and BIDS metadata contracts

Every neuroimaging operation—from cross-modal EPI-to-T1w registration and susceptibility distortion unwarping in fMRIPrep to 3D keypoint alignment in BrainMorph—depends on mapping discrete 0-indexed voxel arrays into continuous physical millimeter coordinates (RAS+) and reading pulse-sequence timing from BIDS JSON sidecars. Stripping NIfTI affines (resetting to an identity matrix), flipping array axes without post-multiplying the affine by the corresponding reflection-translation matrix, or ignoring sform/qform shear divergence silently swaps cerebral hemispheres or shifts coordinates by tens of millimeters without altering image intensity histograms. Mastering the 4x4 homogeneous affine decomposition and header precedence rules forces explicit verification of handedness (sign of det(M)), voxel volume (|det(M)|), and BIDS metadata completeness before running any downstream model.

[The brain imaging data structure, a format for organizing and describing outputs of neuroimaging experiments](https://doi.org/10.1038/sdata.2016.44). *Scientific Data*. Cited by 1,988 (OpenAlex); MATCH.  
  Defines the Brain Imaging Data Structure (BIDS) standard that pairs NIfTI volumes with machine-readable JSON metadata sidecars for acquisition timing and phase-encoding polarity.
- **Standard tool.** Li X, Morgan PS, Ashburner J, Smith J, Rorden C (2016). [The first step for neuroimaging data analysis: DICOM to NIfTI conversion](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jneumeth.2016.03.001). *Journal of Neuroscience Methods*. Cited by 1,169 (OpenAlex); MATCH.  
  Establishes how dcm2niix converts raw scanner DICOM headers into NIfTI-1 qform/sform affine matrices and BIDS JSON sidecars while handling gantry tilt and slice orientation.
- **State of the art.** Esteban O, Markiewicz CJ, Blair RW, Moodie CA, Isik AI, Erramuzpe A, Kent JD, Goncalves M, DuPre E, Snyder M, Oya H, Ghosh SS, Wright J, Durnez J, Poldrack RA, Gorgolewski KJ (2019). ### PR02 · [Estimate alignment, then choose how to sample](../../notebooks/01_processing/02_registration_resampling.ipynb)

*Technique:* Affine image registration, similarity cost functions, backward pull resampling, and interpolation transfer functions

Aligning multi-modal and longitudinal brain scans requires decoupling transformation estimation (via intensity cost functions like Mutual Information or closed-form keypoint least squares) from grid resampling. Forward ('push') coordinate rounding creates unvisited grid holes and collisions, interpolating discrete parcellation masks with linear or spline kernels fabricates invalid anatomical label IDs along ROI boundaries, and applying sequential resamplings across pipeline stages compounds low-pass spatial filtering that biases smoothness-dependent statistical inference. Understanding backward ('pull') sampling, kernel frequency responses, and single-shot composed transformations is essential for building artifact-free registration workflows in FSL FLIRT, ANTs, fMRIPrep, and BrainMorph.

- **Foundational.** Maes F, Collignon A, Vandermeulen D, Marchal G, Suetens P (1997). [Multimodality image registration by maximization of mutual information](https://doi.org/10.1109/42.563664). *IEEE Transactions on Medical Imaging*. Cited by 4,560 (OpenAlex); MATCH.  
  Introduces mutual information maximization for accurate cross-modality 3D brain image registration without requiring linear intensity relationships.
- **Standard tool.** Jenkinson M, Smith S (2001). [A global optimisation method for robust affine registration of brain images](https://doi.org/10.1016/s1361-8415%2801%2900036-6). *Medical Image Analysis*. Cited by 7,273 (OpenAlex); MATCH.  
  Defines the multi-scale global optimization method underlying FSL FLIRT for robust 6-DOF rigid and 12-DOF affine brain registration.
- **State of the art.** Hoffmann M, Billot B, Greve DN, Iglesias JE, Fischl B, Dalca AV (2022). [SynthMorph: Learning Contrast-Invariant Registration Without Acquired Images](https://doi.org/10.1109/tmi.2021.3116879). *IEEE Transactions on Medical Imaging*. Cited by 205 (OpenAlex); MATCH.  
  Demonstrates contrast-invariant deep learning image registration trained entirely on synthetic label-derived images using spatial transformer pull resampling.

### PR03 · [Read a deformation field and detect folding](../../notebooks/01_processing/03_nonlinear_warps.ipynb)

*Technique:* Nonlinear deformable registration, Jacobian determinant topology auditing, spatial regularization, and stationary velocity field integration

Nonlinear registration aligns subject-specific cortical folding and subcortical morphology to a common template using millions of displacement parameters, making the optimization inherently ill-posed without spatial regularization. An unregularized warp can achieve deceptively high intensity similarity or regional Dice overlap while folding tissue over itself (non-positive Jacobian determinant, J(x) <= 0), destroying anatomical topology and invalidating downstream cortical surface projection or morphometry. Computing the deformation gradient tensor F(x) = I + grad u(x), auditing log-Jacobian maps and folding percentages, and integrating stationary velocity fields via scaling-and-squaring (phi = exp(v)) are mandatory to guarantee invertible, diffeomorphic transformations.

- **Foundational.** Ashburner J (2007). [A fast diffeomorphic image registration algorithm](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2007.07.007). *NeuroImage*. Cited by 8,480 (OpenAlex); MATCH.  
  Introduces the DARTEL stationary velocity field parameterization and scaling-and-squaring ODE integration for diffeomorphic brain registration.
- **Standard tool.** Avants BB, Epstein CL, Grossman M, Gee JC (2008). [Symmetric diffeomorphic image registration with cross-correlation: Evaluating automated labeling of elderly and neurodegenerative brain](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.media.2007.06.004). *Medical Image Analysis*. Cited by 6,206 (OpenAlex); MATCH.  
  Establishes Symmetric Normalization (SyN) in ANTs for large-deformation diffeomorphic registration with cross-correlation.
- **State of the art.** Balakrishnan G, Zhao A, Sabuncu MR, Guttag J, Dalca AV (2019). [VoxelMorph: A Learning Framework for Deformable Medical Image Registration](https://doi.org/10.1109/tmi.2019.2897538). *IEEE Transactions on Medical Imaging*. Cited by 2,175 (OpenAlex); MATCH.  
  Formulates unsupervised deep learning deformable registration (VoxelMorph) combining image similarity losses with spatial gradient regularization and Jacobian folding evaluation.
- **Critical evaluation.** Klein A, Andersson J, Ardekani BA, Ashburner J, Avants B, Chiang MC, Christensen GE, Collins DL, Gee J, Hellier P, Song JH, Jenkinson M, Lepage C, Rueckert D, Thompson P, Vercauteren T, Woods RP, Mann JJ, Parsey RV (2009). [Evaluation of 14 nonlinear deformation algorithms applied to human brain MRI registration](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2008.12.037). *NeuroImage*. Cited by 2,339 (OpenAlex); MATCH.  
  Benchmarks 14 nonlinear deformation algorithms across labeled human brain MRI datasets, demonstrating the superiority of diffeomorphic methods such as SyN and DARTEL.

### PR04 · [Separate smooth intensity bias from tissue contrast](../../notebooks/01_processing/04_bias_field.ipynb)

*Technique:* Bias-field (RF intensity non-uniformity) modeling, homomorphic log-filtering, and N4ITK correction

Radiofrequency transmit (B1+) standing-wave interference and phased-array receive (B1-) coil sensitivity drop-off modulate true tissue contrast by a smooth multiplicative gain field B(x) that scales both class means and within-tissue variances (proportionally to mu_c^2). Left uncorrected, low-frequency shading causes white matter in dimly illuminated regions to fall below gray matter intensities in bright peripheral regions, breaking global thresholds, Gaussian mixture models, and registration cost functions. Transforming intensities into the log domain (homomorphic filtering) and applying iterative histogram sharpening with B-spline basis constraints (N4ITK) or generative bias randomization (SynthSeg) removes coil shading while enforcing scale separation so anatomical contrast and focal lesions are not erased.

- **Foundational.** Sled JG, Zijdenbos AP, Evans AC (1998). [A nonparametric method for automatic correction of intensity nonuniformity in MRI data](https://doi.org/10.1109/42.668698). *IEEE Transactions on Medical Imaging*. Cited by 4,858 (OpenAlex); MATCH.  
  Establishes the nonparametric N3 method that estimates smooth multiplicative bias fields via iterative log-intensity histogram deconvolution.
- **Standard tool.** Tustison NJ, Avants BB, Cook PA, Zheng Y, Egan A, Yushkevich PA, Gee JC (2010). [N4ITK: Improved N3 Bias Correction](https://doi.org/10.1109/tmi.2010.2046908). *IEEE Transactions on Medical Imaging*. Cited by 6,601 (OpenAlex); MATCH.  
  Introduces N4ITK, replacing N3's spline smoothing with a fast hierarchical B-spline approximation and improved iterative convergence used across ANTs and fMRIPrep.
- **State of the art.** Iglesias JE, Billot B, Balbastre Y, Magdamo C, Arnold SE, Das S, Edlow BL, Alexander DC, Golland P, Fischl B (2023). [SynthSR: A public AI tool to turn heterogeneous clinical brain scans into high-resolution T1-weighted images for 3D morphometry](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.add3607). *Science Advances*. Cited by 225 (OpenAlex); MATCH.  
  Demonstrates how synthetic domain randomization over aggressive bias fields and resolutions enables turning heterogeneous clinical MRI scans into clean 1-mm isotropic T1w volumes.
- **Review.** Vovk U, Pernus F, Likar B (2007). [A Review of Methods for Correction of Intensity Inhomogeneity in MRI](https://doi.org/10.1109/tmi.2006.891486). *IEEE Transactions on Medical Imaging*. Cited by 856 (OpenAlex); MATCH.  
  Provides a comprehensive taxonomy and critical comparison of prospective and retrospective MRI intensity inhomogeneity correction methods.

### PR05 · [A brain mask defines what the analysis can see](../../notebooks/01_processing/05_brain_extraction.ipynb)

*Technique:* Brain extraction (skull stripping), boundary distance metrics (HD95, ASSD), and downstream morphometric/registration error cascades

Brain extraction defines the spatial domain for downstream bias correction, tissue segmentation, inter-modal coregistration, and cortical surface reconstruction. Because the cerebral cortex is a thin outer ribbon (~2-4 mm) surrounding a large interior volume, whole-brain volumetric Dice is mathematically insensitive to superficial cortical erosion (over-stripping that causes fake cortical atrophy in FreeSurfer/FastSurfer) as well as localized dural, marrow, or orbital retention (under-stripping that pulls pial surfaces outward, biases intensity clustering thresholds, and shifts registration centers of mass). Auditing brain masks therefore requires multi-slice contour overlays alongside surface distance metrics (HD95, ASSD) and compartment-specific cortical ribbon recall.

- **Foundational.** Smith SM (2002). [Fast robust automated brain extraction](https://doi.org/10.1002/hbm.10062). *Human Brain Mapping*. Cited by 10,954 (OpenAlex); MATCH.  
  Introduces FSL's Brain Extraction Tool (BET), modeling the brain surface as a tessellated mesh that evolves outward under smoothness and local intensity-threshold forces.
- **State of the art.** Isensee F, Schell M, Pflueger I, Brugnara G, Bonekamp D, Neuberger U, Wick A, Schlemmer HP, Heiland S, Wick W, Bendszus M, Maier-Hein KH, Kickingereder P (2019). [Automated brain extraction of multisequence MRI using artificial neural networks](https://doi.org/10.1002/hbm.24750). *Human Brain Mapping*. Cited by 763 (OpenAlex); MATCH.  
  Establishes HD-BET, demonstrating that 3D U-Net brain extraction generalizes across multisequence clinical MRI scans and pathological brains.
- **Critical evaluation.** Iglesias JE, Liu CY, Thompson PM, Tu Z (2011). [Robust Brain Extraction Across Datasets and Comparison With Publicly Available Methods](https://doi.org/10.1109/tmi.2011.2138152). *IEEE Transactions on Medical Imaging*. Cited by 612 (OpenAlex); MATCH.

### PR06 · [Labels, posterior probabilities and partial volume are different](../../notebooks/01_processing/06_tissue_segmentation.ipynb)

*Technique:* Probabilistic tissue segmentation, Gaussian Mixture Models with EM, Markov Random Field priors, and partial-volume estimation

Brain tissue segmentation partitions structural MRI into cerebrospinal fluid (CSF), gray matter (GM), and white matter (WM) for morphometry and surface reconstruction, but finite voxel resolution causes boundary voxels to contain mixtures of multiple tissues (partial volume effects, PVE). Because CSF (~30) and WM (~110) average to the intensity of pure GM (~75) on T1w MRI, pointwise classifiers without spatial neighborhood priors (such as FSL FAST's HMRF/Potts model or 3D contextual CNNs like SynthSeg) fabricate a false rim of gray matter around the ventricles, while winner-take-all argmax thresholding systematically erases thin cortical ribbons on thick-slice scans. Distinguishing discrete MAP labels, Bayesian posterior probabilities, and physical partial-volume fractions is essential for unbiased regional volume estimation.

- **Foundational.** Ashburner J, Friston KJ (2005). [Unified segmentation](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2005.02.018). *NeuroImage*. Cited by 7,643 (OpenAlex); MATCH.  
  Introduces SPM's Unified Segmentation generative framework that jointly optimizes Gaussian mixture tissue classification, smooth bias field correction, and deformable tissue probability map registration.
- **Standard tool.** Zhang Y, Brady M, Smith S (2001). [Segmentation of brain MR images through a hidden Markov random field model and the expectation-maximization algorithm](https://doi.org/10.1109/42.906424). *IEEE Transactions on Medical Imaging*. Cited by 7,429 (OpenAlex); MATCH.  
  Formulates the hidden Markov random field (HMRF) and EM algorithm coupled with bias-field correction that powers FSL FAST.
- **State of the art.** Henschel L, Conjeti S, Estrada S, Diers K, Fischl B, Reuter M (2020). [FastSurfer - A fast and accurate deep learning based neuroimaging pipeline](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2020.117012). *NeuroImage*. Cited by 713 (OpenAlex); MATCH. Course library: [PP07](processing.md#pp07).  
  Demonstrates whole-brain deep learning parcellation (FastSurferCNN) and cortical surface reconstruction with sub-millimeter thickness fidelity.
- **State of the art.** Billot B, Magdamo C, Cheng Y, Arnold SE, Das S, Iglesias JE (2023). ### PR07 · [Measure volume before making an atrophy claim](../../notebooks/01_processing/07_morphometry_volume_jacobian.ipynb)

*Technique:* Volumetric morphometry, VBM Jacobian modulation, and Total Intracranial Volume (TIV) adjustment

Structural MRI segmentation masks only become valid biological volume measurements when calibrated by physical voxel volume (|det(M)|), modulated by the nonlinear warp Jacobian determinant in template space (Voxel-Based Morphometry), and adjusted for premorbid head size (Total Intracranial Volume, TIV/eTIV). Without these steps, scanner resolution differences alter raw voxel counts up to 9-fold, nonlinear registration erases true regional atrophy in unmodulated template maps, and sampling imbalances in head size can reverse the sign of group atrophy comparisons (Simpson's Paradox). Researchers must choose between native-space ROI integration vs. Jacobian-modulated VBM, linear control-slope residualization vs. proportional/allometric TIV scaling, and cross-sectional vs. longitudinal within-subject designs.

- **Foundational.** Ashburner J, Friston KJ (2000). [Voxel-Based Morphometry—The Methods](https://doi.org/10.1006/nimg.2000.0582). *NeuroImage*. Cited by 8,776 (OpenAlex); MATCH.  
  Introduces the foundational Voxel-Based Morphometry (VBM) framework for whole-brain voxel-wise statistical comparison of regional tissue probabilities in stereotaxic space.
- **Foundational.** Good CD, Johnsrude IS, Ashburner J, Henson RN, Friston KJ, Frackowiak RS (2001). [A Voxel-Based Morphometric Study of Ageing in 465 Normal Adult Human Brains](https://doi.org/10.1006/nimg.2001.0786). *NeuroImage*. Cited by 4,633 (OpenAlex); MATCH.  
  Formalizes 'optimized VBM' with Jacobian determinant modulation so that nonlinearly normalized tissue probability maps conserve native-space physical tissue volume rather than measuring relative concentration.
- **Standard tool.** Gaser C, Dahnke R, Thompson PM, Kurth F, Luders E, the Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (2024). [CAT: a computational anatomy toolbox for the analysis of structural MRI data](https://doi.org/10.1093/gigascience/giae049). *GigaScience*. Cited by 922 (OpenAlex); MATCH.  
  Describes the modern Computational Anatomy Toolbox (CAT12) for automated voxel-based and surface-based morphometry, Jacobian modulation, TIV estimation, and longitudinal atrophy processing.
- **Critical evaluation.** Barnes J, Ridgway GR, Bartlett J, Henley SM, Lehmann M, Hobbs N, Clarkson MJ, MacManus DG, Ourselin S, Fox NC (2010). [Head size, age and gender adjustment in MRI studies: a necessary nuisance?](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2010.06.025). *NeuroImage*. Cited by 538 (OpenAlex); MATCH.  
  Evaluates head-size (TIV), age, and sex covariate adjustment strategies across cortical and subcortical morphometry, demonstrating why non-zero intercepts bias naive proportional division (V/TIV).

### PR08 · [Surfaces are meshes, not thin voxel masks](../../notebooks/01_processing/08_surfaces_topology.ipynb)

*Technique:* Cortical surface triangular meshes, Euler-Poincare topology verification, cortical thickness, and geodesic smoothing

The human cerebral cortex is a tightly folded 2D sheet where opposing banks of a sulcus are separated by only 2-3 mm across CSF in 3D Euclidean space but 20-30 mm along the cortical ribbon. ### PR18 · [Fit a tensor and interpret FA and MD within its limits](../../notebooks/01_processing/18_diffusion_tensor.ipynb)

Diffusion Tensor Imaging (Basser et al., 1994) is the most widely deployed microstructural MRI model in clinical and cognitive neuroscience, summarizing directional water diffusivity via scalar invariants such as Fractional Anisotropy (FA) and Mean Diffusivity (MD). Without understanding heteroscedastic log-domain noise (Salvador et al., 2005; Veraart et al., 2013), unweighted OLS fits over-weight noisy high-attenuation directions, and without checking physical MD units, scale-invariant FA masks 1000x b-value unit errors. Crucially, because 60%–90% of white-matter voxels contain crossing fibers (Jeurissen et al., 2013), a single tensor collapses into an oblate ellipsoid with reduced FA, forcing researchers to use Westin shape indices or multi-fiber models rather than misinterpreting lower FA as demyelination (Jones et al., 2013; Figley et al., 2022).

- **Foundational.** Basser PJ, Mattiello J, LeBihan D (1994). [MR diffusion tensor spectroscopy and imaging](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0006-3495%2894%2980775-1). *Biophysical Journal*. Cited by 6,140 (OpenAlex); MATCH.  
  Formulates the 3x3 symmetric diffusion tensor model and linear B-matrix estimation from directional Stejskal-Tanner measurements.
- **Foundational.** Pierpaoli C, Basser PJ (1996). [Toward a quantitative assessment of diffusion anisotropy](https://doi.org/10.1002/mrm.1910360612). *Magnetic Resonance in Medicine*. Cited by 2,452 (OpenAlex); MATCH.  
  Defines Fractional Anisotropy (FA) and characterizes how thermal noise induces eigenvalue sorting bias in anisotropic and isotropic tissues.
- **Critical evaluation.** Jones DK, Knösche TR, Turner R (2013). [White matter integrity, fiber count, and other fallacies: The do's and don'ts of diffusion MRI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2012.06.081). *NeuroImage*. Cited by 2,478 (OpenAlex); MATCH.  
  Explains why scalar FA is non-specific to myelination or axonal integrity because crossing fibers, partial volume, and acquisition noise alter eigenvalue dispersion.
- **Critical evaluation.** Figley CR, Uddin MN, Wong K, Kornelsen J, Puig J, Figley TD (2022). [Potential Pitfalls of Using Fractional Anisotropy, Axial Diffusivity, and Radial Diffusivity as Biomarkers of Cerebral White Matter Microstructure](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2021.799576). *Frontiers in Neuroscience*. Cited by 255 (OpenAlex); MATCH.  
  Demonstrates how crossing fibers and partial-volume effects confound voxelwise FA, AD, and RD interpretations in clinical and neuroimaging studies.

### PR19 · [A streamline is a model trajectory, not an axon](../../notebooks/01_processing/19_crossing_fibers_tractography.ipynb)

*Technique:* Crossing-fiber spherical deconvolution (CSD / fODF) and deterministic/probabilistic streamline tractography

Because most white-matter voxels contain crossing or fanning axon bundles, single-tensor tractography terminates prematurely or follows false diagonal averages, motivating Fiber Orientation Distribution Function (fODF) estimation via Constrained Spherical Deconvolution (Tournier et al., 2007; Jeurissen et al., 2014). However, as the ISMRM 2015 Tractography Challenge demonstrated (Maier-Hein et al., 2017, PP06), even exact local fiber orientations cannot prevent streamlines from taking false exit branches after merging into shared anatomical bottlenecks such as the internal capsule or temporal stem. Understanding ODE integration step-size errors, angular stopping criteria, anatomical priors (ACT), and tractogram filtering (SIFT2) is essential before interpreting streamline counts as structural connectivity.

Cited by 2,363 (OpenAlex); MATCH.  
  Establishes Constrained Spherical Deconvolution (CSD) to resolve multiple intra-voxel fiber orientations by deconvolving the single-fiber response kernel under non-negativity constraints.
- **Foundational.** Behrens TEJ, Berg HJ, Jbabdi S, Rushworth MFS, Woolrich MW (2007). [Probabilistic diffusion tractography with multiple fibre orientations: What can we gain?](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2006.09.018). *NeuroImage*. Cited by 3,637 (OpenAlex); MATCH.

### D01 · [The question is an estimand, not a button](../../notebooks/02_design/01_questions_estimands_causality.ipynb)

*Technique:* Causal estimands, potential outcomes, DAG identification, and inverse-probability / standardization adjustment

Every neuroimaging analysis—from multi-team task-fMRI pipelines (NARPS) to biobank brain-wide association studies (UK Biobank, ABCD)—implicitly targets a specific causal or descriptive estimand before any regression or machine-learning estimator is chosen. Without explicitly specifying the unit-level potential outcomes, target population, and confounder set on a causal DAG, researchers conflate analytical flexibility that changes the scientific question (e.g., conditioning on colliders or altering parametric modulators) with mere estimator variance, and risk severe bias when covariate overlap (positivity) fails across sites or clinical groups.

- **Foundational.** Rubin DB (1974). [Estimating causal effects of treatments in randomized and nonrandomized studies.](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0037350). *Journal of Educational Psychology*. Cited by 9,621 (OpenAlex); MATCH.  
  Formalizes causal effects as comparisons of potential outcomes Y(1) and Y(0) on a common set of units, establishing why observational comparisons require explicit assignment-mechanism assumptions.
- **Foundational.** ROSENBAUM PR, RUBIN DB (1983). [The central role of the propensity score in observational studies for causal effects](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/70.1.41). *Biometrika*. Cited by 31,600 (OpenAlex); MATCH.  
  Introduces the propensity score e(X)=P(A=1|X) and proves that conditioning or weighting on e(X) suffices to remove confounding from observed covariates X under strong ignorability and positivity.
- **Critical evaluation.** Lundberg I, Johnson R, Stewart BM (2021). [What Is Your Estimand? Defining the Target Quantity Connects Statistical Evidence to Theory](https://doi.org/10.1177/00031224211004187). *American Sociological Review*. Cited by 379 (OpenAlex); MATCH.  
  Shows why researchers must state the unit-specific quantity, target population, and counterfactual comparison before choosing an estimator or regression specification.
- **Review.** Hernán MA, Robins JM (2006). [Estimating causal effects from epidemiological data](https://doi.org/10.1136/jech.2004.029496). *Journal of Epidemiology and Community Health*. Cited by 1,063 (OpenAlex); MATCH.  
  Clearly links potential-outcome estimands, exchangeability, positivity, and consistency to inverse-probability weighting and standardization in observational studies.

### D02 · [Sampling, missingness, and the people behind rows](../../notebooks/02_design/02_sampling_missingness_units.ipynb)

*Technique:* Hierarchical sampling units, pseudoreplication, missingness mechanisms (MCAR/MAR/MNAR), collider selection bias, and inverse-probability of selection weighting (IPSW)

Large-scale neuroimaging cohorts (ABCD, HCP, UK Biobank) combine multi-run, multi-visit, and family-clustered observations with stringent quality-control exclusions (especially head-motion scrubbing) that systematically remove younger participants and individuals with higher clinical symptom severity. Treating repeated scans or twin pairs as independent rows inflates effective sample size by the Kish design effect and causes high false-positive rates (pseudoreplication), while complete-case analysis after motion-related exclusions conditions on a selection collider or MAR/MNAR mechanism that biases brain-behavior estimates unless hierarchical models and inverse-probability retention weights are used.

- **Foundational.** RUBIN DB (1976). [Inference and missing data](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/63.3.581). *Biometrika*. Cited by 9,872 (OpenAlex); MATCH.  
  Defines the MCAR, MAR, and MNAR missing-data taxonomy and gives the exact conditions under which missingness mechanisms can be ignored for likelihood or frequentist inference.
- **Foundational.** Hernán MA, Hernández-Díaz S, Robins JM (2004). [A Structural Approach to Selection Bias](https://doi.org/10.1097/01.ede.0000135174.63482.43). *Epidemiology*. Cited by 2,851 (OpenAlex); MATCH.  
  Unifies selection bias across study designs as conditioning on a common effect (collider) of exposure and outcome on a causal DAG, such as analyzing only retained low-motion scans.
- **State of the art.** Nebel MB, Lidstone DE, Wang L, Benkeser D, Mostofsky SH, Risk BB (2022). [Accounting for motion in resting-state fMRI: What part of the spectrum are we characterizing in autism spectrum disorder?](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2022.119296). *NeuroImage*. Cited by 48 (OpenAlex); MATCH.  
  Treats head-motion quality-control exclusion in developmental and clinical fMRI as an informative missing-data problem and applies inverse-probability / doubly robust weighting to reduce selection bias.
- **Critical evaluation.** Lazic SE (2010). [The problem of pseudoreplication in neuroscientific studies: is it affecting your analysis?](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2202-11-5). *BMC Neuroscience*. Cited by 376 (OpenAlex); MATCH.  
  Demonstrates how treating repeated measurements, runs, or related observations as independent sample size N inflates degrees of freedom and Type I error across neuroscience.

### D03 · [Reliability is about differences between people](../../notebooks/02_design/03_reliability_measurement.ipynb)

*Technique:* Classical test theory, intraclass correlation coefficients ICC(2,1) vs ICC(3,1), Spearman-Brown multi-session aggregation, and Spearman attenuation correction

A neuroimaging measure that yields a large group-level activation t-statistic can simultaneously fail as an individual-differences biomarker if between-subject variance is small relative to within-subject measurement error—the reliability paradox demonstrated across common task-fMRI paradigms (mean ICC ≈ 0.397; Elliott et al., 2020) and resting-state edges (mean ICC ≈ 0.29; Noble et al., 2019). Choosing between consistency ICC(3,1) and absolute-agreement ICC(2,1) determines whether systematic scanner or longitudinal drift is penalized, while Spearman's attenuation formula shows how reliability ceilings directly cap observable brain-behavior correlations and inflate required sample sizes.

- **Foundational.** McGraw KO, Wong SP (1996). [Forming inferences about some intraclass correlation coefficients.](https://doi.org/10.1037/1082-989X.1.1.30). *Psychological Methods*. Cited by 6,841 (OpenAlex); MATCH.

- **Critical evaluation.** Hedge C, Powell G, Sumner P (2018). [The reliability paradox: Why robust cognitive tasks do not produce reliable individual differences](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13428-017-0935-1). *Behavior Research Methods*. Cited by 1,598 (OpenAlex); MATCH.  
  Explains the reliability paradox: experimental tasks selected to minimize between-subject variance tau^2 yield strong group-level t-statistics but poor individual-differences reliability.
- **Review.** Noble S, Scheinost D, Constable RT (2019). [A decade of test-retest reliability of functional connectivity: A systematic review and meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2019.116157). *NeuroImage*. Cited by 661 (OpenAlex); MATCH.

### D04 · [Plan sample size from effects and precision](../../notebooks/02_design/04_power_precision.ipynb)

*Technique:* Statistical power, Accuracy in Parameter Estimation (AIPE), Type M/S error design analysis, and group-sequential alpha-spending boundaries

Because reproducible univariate brain-wide association effects are typically small (|r| ≈ 0.06–0.16 even in the top 1%; Marek et al., 2022), traditional neuroimaging studies with n = 20–50 have low power and suffer severe Type M exaggeration (winner's curse) and Type S sign errors whenever selection is conditioned on p < 0.05. Prospective design analysis (Gelman & Carlin, 2014), confidence-interval width planning (AIPE), and pre-specified group-sequential alpha-spending boundaries (Pocock, 1977; O'Brien & Fleming, 1979; Lakens, 2022) allow researchers to size cohorts for precision and conduct interim analyses without inflating Type I error.

- **Foundational.** Button KS, Ioannidis JPA, Mokrysz C, Nosek BA, Flint J, Robinson ESJ, Munafò MR (2013). [Power failure: why small sample size undermines the reliability of neuroscience](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn3475). *Nature Reviews Neuroscience*. Cited by 8,074 (OpenAlex); MATCH.  
  Quantifies low statistical power across neuroscience and shows how small samples reduce positive predictive value and inflate significant effect estimates via the winner's curse.
- **Foundational.** Gelman A, Carlin J (2014). [Beyond Power Calculations](https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691614551642). *Perspectives on Psychological Science*. Cited by 1,410 (OpenAlex); MATCH.  
  Defines Type M (exaggeration ratio) and Type S (sign error) metrics via design analysis against realistic external effect-size priors rather than post-hoc observed effects.
- **Standard tool.** O'Brien PC, Fleming TR (1979). [A Multiple Testing Procedure for Clinical Trials](https://doi.org/10.2307/2530245). *Biometrics*. Cited by 3,371 (OpenAlex); MATCH.  
  Introduces conservative early-look group-sequential stopping boundaries that preserve overall family-wise Type I error alpha while maintaining near-fixed-sample power at the final analysis.
- **Review.** Lakens D (2022). [Sample Size Justification](https://doi.org/10.1525/collabra.33267). *Collabra: Psychology*. Cited by 1,731 (OpenAlex); MATCH.  
  Provides a modern framework for justifying sample size via power for a smallest effect size of interest, accuracy in parameter estimation (AIPE), and sequential designs.

### D06 · [Let the response shape vary: FIR versus a fixed HRF](../../notebooks/02_design/06_fir_hrf_misspecification.ipynb)

*Technique:* Hemodynamic response basis sets (canonical HRF, Taylor temporal/dispersion derivatives, and Finite Impulse Response deconvolution) and the bias-variance trade-off

- **State of the art.** Prince JS, Charest I, Kurzawski JW, Pyles JA, Tarr MJ, Kay KN (2022). [Improving the accuracy of single-trial fMRI response estimates using GLMsingle](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.77599). *eLife*. Cited by 190 (OpenAlex); MATCH.

- **Critical evaluation.** Handwerker DA, Ollinger JM, D'Esposito M (2004). [Variation of BOLD hemodynamic responses across subjects and brain regions and their effects on statistical analyses](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2003.11.029). *NeuroImage*. Cited by 1,039 (OpenAlex); MATCH.

### D07 · [Efficiency belongs to a contrast](../../notebooks/02_design/07_design_efficiency.ipynb)

  Derives the fundamental mathematical trade-off between detection power (maximized by blocked designs) and HRF estimation efficiency (maximized by randomized rapid event-related designs).

### D08 · [Factorial questions and the meaning of an interaction](../../notebooks/02_design/08_factorial_confounding.ipynb)

*Technique:* Factorial experimental designs, treatment vs sum/effect contrast coding, multi-site cell-imbalance confounding, and direct interaction testing

Neuroimaging studies routinely cross cognitive conditions, clinical diagnoses, and scanner sites in factorial designs to test modulation beyond simple cognitive subtraction (Friston et al., 1996). Misinterpreting dummy-coded coefficients as marginal main effects in the presence of an interaction (Schad et al., 2020), pooling unbalanced site-by-diagnosis cells without stratification or harmonization (Fortin et al., 2018), or claiming an interaction because one subgroup has p < 0.05 and another has p > 0.05—an error found in nearly half of surveyed neuroscience papers (Gelman & Stern, 2006; Nieuwenhuis et al., 2011)—leads directly to false scientific conclusions.

- **Foundational.** Friston K, Price C, Fletcher P, Moore C, Frackowiak R, Dolan R (1996). [The Trouble with Cognitive Subtraction](https://doi.org/10.1006/nimg.1996.0033). *NeuroImage*. Cited by 552 (OpenAlex); MATCH.

### D09 · [From coefficients to a defensible contrast](../../notebooks/02_design/09_glm_contrasts_inference.ipynb)

*Technique:* General linear model (GLM) contrast specification, covariance propagation, and t/F/z inference

### D10 · [Correlated residuals and prewhitening](../../notebooks/02_design/10_temporal_noise_prewhitening.ipynb)

*Technique:* fMRI temporal autocorrelation modeling, prewhitening, and generalized least squares (GLS)

First-level fMRI time series exhibit strong positive autocorrelation from slow hemodynamic sluggishness, physiological pulsations, and scanner drift (Purdon & Weisskoff, 1998; Woolrich et al., 2001). Valid inference requires symmetrical Cholesky prewhitening of both the data y and the design matrix X using an expressive noise covariance model (such as SPM FAST or AFNI 3dREMLfit ARMA(1,1) at short multiband TRs; Corbin et al., 2018; Olszowy et al., 2019).

  Establishes how physiological noise and temporal autocorrelation inflate voxel-level false-positive rates in fMRI when unmodeled.
- **Standard tool.** Woolrich MW, Ripley BD, Brady M, Smith SM (2001). [Temporal Autocorrelation in Univariate Linear Modeling of FMRI Data](https://doi.org/10.1006/nimg.2001.0931). *NeuroImage*. Cited by 3,058 (OpenAlex); MATCH.

### D11 · [People, runs, visits, and hierarchical uncertainty](../../notebooks/02_design/11_group_hierarchy_repeated.ipynb)

*Technique:* Multilevel mixed-effects modeling, repeated-measures hierarchy, and empirical Bayes shrinkage

Neuroimaging datasets are inherently hierarchical, nesting trials within runs, runs within participants, and participants within scanner sites (Beckmann et al., 2003; Woolrich et al., 2004; Chen et al., 2012). - **Standard tool.** Woolrich MW, Behrens TE, Beckmann CF, Jenkinson M, Smith SM (2004). [Multilevel linear modelling for FMRI group analysis using Bayesian inference](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2003.12.023). *NeuroImage*. Cited by 1,829 (OpenAlex); MATCH.

- **State of the art.** Chen G, Pine DS, Brotman MA, Smith AR, Cox RW, Taylor PA et al. (2022). [Hyperbolic trade-off: The importance of balancing trial and subject sample sizes in neuroimaging](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2021.118786). *NeuroImage*. Cited by 76 (OpenAlex); MATCH.

### D12 · [Permutation tests must preserve the experiment](../../notebooks/02_design/12_permutation_exchangeability.ipynb)

*Technique:* Nonparametric permutation inference, exchangeability blocks, and cluster/subject bootstrap resampling

When Gaussian random field assumptions fail in neuroimaging, nonparametric permutation tests (in FSL randomise and PALM) provide exact or conservative false-positive control—provided the rearranged configurations are exchangeable under the null hypothesis (Nichols & Holmes, 2002; Winkler et al., 2014). Shuffling rows across repeated runs, twin families, or scanner sites violates exchangeability (requiring multi-level block permutation; Winkler et al., 2015), and omitting the +1 identity count in Monte Carlo permutation p-values yields invalid p = 0 claims (Phipson & Smyth, 2010). Likewise, bootstrap resampling must resample independent participants rather than flat rows and does not make the sample mean robust to extreme outliers.

- **Foundational.** Nichols TE, Holmes AP (2002). [Nonparametric permutation tests for functional neuroimaging: A primer with examples](https://doi.org/10.1002/hbm.1058). *Human Brain Mapping*. Cited by 6,549 (OpenAlex); MATCH.  
  Establishes the nonparametric permutation testing framework and exchangeability principles for functional neuroimaging.
- **Standard tool.** Winkler AM, Ridgway GR, Webster MA, Smith SM, Nichols TE (2014). [Permutation inference for the general linear model](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2014.01.060). *NeuroImage*. Cited by 3,889 (OpenAlex); MATCH.  
  Provides the definitive formulation of permutation inference for the GLM, including Freedman-Lane nuisance residualization and exchangeability blocks in FSL randomise/PALM.
- **State of the art.** Winkler AM, Renaud O, Smith SM, Nichols TE (2020). [Permutation inference for canonical correlation analysis](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2020.117065). *NeuroImage*. Cited by 3 (OpenAlex); MATCH.  
  Extends block-exchangeable and nuisance-adjusted permutation inference to multivariate canonical correlation analysis in large neuroimaging cohorts.
- **Critical evaluation.** Phipson B, Smyth GK (2010). [Permutation P-values Should Never Be Zero: Calculating Exact P-values When Permutations Are Randomly Drawn](https://doi.org/10.2202/1544-6115.1585). *Statistical Applications in Genetics and Molecular Biology*. ### D13 · [Voxel families, clusters, and TFCE are different targets](../../notebooks/02_design/13_multiplicity_clusters_tfce.ipynb)

### D14 · [Region selection and evaluation must be independent](../../notebooks/02_design/14_roi_selection_circularity.ipynb)

*Technique:* Circular analysis (double dipping) detection, winner's curse bias, and independent ROI validation

### D15 · [Intervals, Bayesian updating, and meaningful effects](../../notebooks/02_design/15_bayesian_intervals_equivalence.ipynb)

*Technique:* Effect-size estimation, frequentist confidence intervals, conjugate Bayesian updating, and TOST/ROPE equivalence testing

A non-significant p-value (p > 0.05) never proves the absence of an effect; it fails to distinguish a genuinely negligible effect from a noisy, underpowered study (Altman & Bland, 1995; Lakens, 2017).   Provides a comprehensive tutorial on choosing smallest effect sizes of interest (SESOI) and combining NHST with TOST equivalence testing.
- **Standard tool.** Kruschke JK (2018). [Rejecting or Accepting Parameter Values in Bayesian Estimation](https://doi.org/10.1177/2515245918771304). *Advances in Methods and Practices in Psychological Science*. Cited by 781 (OpenAlex); MATCH.

### DS14 · [SVD, PCA, compression, and train-only representations](../../notebooks/03_data_science/14_pca_svd.ipynb)

*Technique:* Principal Component Analysis (PCA), Singular Value Decomposition (SVD), Eckart-Young low-rank approximation, and unsupervised variance vs. supervised signal

PCA and SVD underpin functional connectivity eigenmodes, ICA pre-whitening (FSL MELODIC), representational similarity analysis (PM02), and feature compression prior to clinical prediction. Because SVD orders components purely by unsupervised variance sigma_k^2 / sum(sigma_j^2), global nuisance fluctuations (scanner drift, head size, physiological noise) often dominate the leading PCs while focal clinical or cognitive signals reside in lower-variance components (Jolliffe, 1982). Furthermore, failing to mean-center features before SVD or fitting the PCA basis on pooled train+test data corrupts the subspace and leaks test geometry.

Cited by 720 (OpenAlex); MATCH.

### P04 · [Turn the notebooks into a defensible supervised research plan](../../notebooks/05_projects/04_research_defense.ipynb)

*Technique:* Supervised research plan verification, pseudoreplication auditing, multiverse specification-curve analysis, low-label transfer benchmarking, and Type M power analysis
