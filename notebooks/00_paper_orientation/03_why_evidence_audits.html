Total Variance Across Analytical Pipelines (Multiverse Decomposition)

Decomposes total uncertainty into standard within-pipeline sampling error plus the hidden variance across defensible analysis pipelines.

\[\operatorname{Var}(\hat{\theta}) = \underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\!\left[\operatorname{Var}(\hat{\theta} \mid \pi)\right]}_{\text{Sampling Variance Within Pipeline}} + \underbrace{\operatorname{Var}_{\pi}\!\left(\mathbb{E}[\hat{\theta} \mid \pi]\right)}_{\text{Pipeline / Vibration Variance Across Choices}}\]
Logical Term Role in Equation What This Part Is Doing (Mechanism & Units) What Breaks If Omitted or Mis-Specified (Failure Mode)
\(\operatorname{Var}(\hat{\theta})\)Total Claim UncertaintyTrue total variance of the reported scientific estimate across both participant sampling and analytical branches.Reporting only the first term and pretending the pipeline choice had zero impact.
\(\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[\operatorname{Var}(\hat{\theta} \mid \pi)]\)Conventional Standard Error TermAverage sampling variance printed by software assuming a single fixed pipeline pi.Treating a narrow single-pipeline p-value as proof that the scientific conclusion is robust.
\(\operatorname{Var}_{\pi}(\mathbb{E}[\hat{\theta} \mid \pi])\)Analytical Vibration VarianceDispersion of the expected estimate across valid preprocessing, design, and inference specifications (NARPS).Cherry-picking the single pipeline branch pi* that maximizes statistical significance.

Seminar Framing: Analytical Vibration ("The Multiverse") and Frontier Benchmark Audits

In R01, you saw that a raw scan undergoes a complex chain of spatial and temporal preprocessing steps before analysis. In R02, you saw that sample sizes, phenotype SNR, and train/test boundaries govern whether a result generalizes to a new person. Now, in R03, we confront the final supervisory question before entering the computational labs: How do researcher degrees of freedom and benchmark design choices shape whether a result looks "significant" or "state-of-the-art"?

We pair two landmark studies that every modern neuroimaging researcher must know how to read side by side:

  1. The Analytical Multiverse Audit (PD01): Botvinik-Nezer et al. (NARPS, Nature, 2020), where 70 independent research teams tested the exact same 9 neuroimaging hypotheses on the exact same \(N = 108\) task-fMRI dataset—yielding wide variability in binary "yes/no" significance decisions alongside striking convergence in underlying unthresholded effect maps (connecting directly to Eklund et al., 2016, PD05 on cluster inference).
  2. The Frontier fMRI Foundation Model (PM05): Wang et al. (Omni-fMRI, ICML 2026 / assigned arXiv v1 2601.23090v1), which proposes an "atlas-free" fMRI foundation model using variance-based dynamic patch tokenization and scale-aware masked autoencoding—providing a live 2026 test case for auditing tokenization assumptions, preprocessing dependencies, and table counterexamples (connecting to OpenMind, Wald et al., 2025, PM04).

Read this pair first: Multi-Team Analytical Variability vs. Universal fMRI Foundation Models

  • Essential Foundational Paper (PD01): Botvinik-Nezer et al., Variability in the analysis of a single neuroimaging dataset by many teams—NARPS (Nature, 2020; 582:84–88). Full paper · PD01 coursework card.
  • Recent Frontier Method (PM05): Wang et al., Omni-fMRI: A Universal Atlas-Free fMRI Foundation Model (listed in ICML 2026). Assigned arXiv v1, 30 January 2026 · PM05 coursework card and publication-status evidence.

First-pass reading targets:

  1. In PD01 (NARPS): Abstract, Figure 1 & caption (fraction of 70 teams reporting a significant effect for each of the 9 hypotheses alongside prediction-market beliefs), Figure 2 & caption (pairwise Spearman rank correlations among the 64 teams that supplied usable unthresholded whole-brain \(Z\)-statistic maps), Results on sources of analytical variation (spatial smoothing, HRF modeling, multiple-testing thresholding), and Discussion on multiverse analysis and data sharing.
  2. In PM05 (Omni-fMRI, assigned arXiv v1 2601.23090v1): Abstract, Figure 2 & Sections 3.1–3.3 (spatiotemporal complexity estimation via local intensity variance, coarse-to-fine dynamic patch subdivision, dual-path multi-scale embedding, and scale-aware Masked Autoencoder pretraining on \(49,497\) fMRI sessions across \(9\) datasets), Table 3 & Sections 4.2–4.5 (downstream regression and classification benchmarks across ABIDE, NKI, and SALD age regression, ABCD and HCP sex classification, and BHRC, PPMI, and ADNI clinical tasks, plus linear probing in Table 6 and Integrated Gradients interpretability in Section 4.5), and Section 5 Conclusion & Limitations (heuristic variance-threshold tokenization).
    • Provenance Rule: Conference listing status (ICML 2026) and the exact manuscript text inspected (arXiv:2601.23090v1, dated 30 Jan 2026) are recorded as separate provenance fields. Numbers in any later revision must not silently overwrite the assigned v1 table audit.

Side-by-Side Forensic Comparison (PD01 vs. PM05 & Audit Strand Context)

Analytical Dimension PD01 · NARPS (Botvinik-Nezer et al., 2020) PM05 · Omni-fMRI (Wang et al., 2026, arXiv v1) Connections to PD05 (Eklund) & PM04 (OpenMind)
Core Problem Addressed Analytical flexibility ("researcher degrees of freedom"): How much do reasonable, expert-chosen fMRI preprocessing and statistical modeling pipelines diverge when testing identical biological hypotheses on identical raw data? Representation bottleneck of fixed brain atlases: Standard fMRI models project 4D voxel data onto fixed anatomical parcellations (e.g., AAL, Schaefer-400), discarding fine voxel geometry and imposing mismatched boundaries across individuals. PD05 (Eklund et al., 2016) showed how parametric cluster-extent thresholding choices (p < 0.01 vs p < 0.001) drastically alter false-positive rates—one of the primary drivers of NARPS divergence
Input Data & Preprocessing Dependency Raw BIDS task-fMRI dataset (\(N = 108\) participants performing a mixed-gambles loss-aversion task); roughly half the teams (\(33/70\)) used standardized fMRIPrep (PP01) derivatives while the rest ran custom FSL/SPM/AFNI pipelines. Preprocessed 4D fMRI volumes (resampled to \(2\text{ mm}\) isotropic, cropped/padded to \(96^3\), temporally interpolated to \(\text{TR} = 0.72\text{ s}\), globally \(z\)-scored) across \(49,497\) sessions from \(9\) pretraining datasets—"atlas-free" means no fixed ROI parcellation is applied, NOT that raw unaligned scanner data are fed in without preprocessing. PM04 (OpenMind, Wald et al., 2025) demonstrates that benchmark wins in 3D SSL heavily depend on matched architectures, patch sizes, and fine-tuning schedules.
Observation Unit & Denominators Fig. 1: \(K = 70\) independent analysis teams reporting binary decisions on 9 hypotheses (\(N = 108\) subjects). Fig. 2: \(K = 64\) teams with usable unthresholded \(Z\)/\(t\) statistical maps (6 teams excluded due to missing, thresholded, or spatially incompatible maps). Pretraining: \(49,497\) fMRI sessions tokenized via variance-based dynamic patching. Downstream (Table 3): Subject/session-level regression (\(\text{MSE} \downarrow\), Pearson \(\text{R} \uparrow\)) and classification (\(\text{AUROC}/\text{ACC} \uparrow\)) across cohorts (mean \(\pm\) SD over \(3\) runs). Always verify the exact denominator before interpreting a percentage or mean.
Key Nuance / Counterexample Inside the Paper Binary Decisions Disagree, Continuous Maps Agree: One hypothesis (#5) reached \(84.3\%\) significant consensus and three (#7–#9) reached \(5.7\%\), while the remaining five (#1–#4, #6) split at \(21.4\%\text{--}37.1\%\); yet Figure 2 shows a large majority core of unthresholded maps are strongly positively correlated (plus a small anti-correlated cluster of teams that mis-specified their GLM regressor). Table 3 Benchmark Exception (SALD Age Regression): While Omni-fMRI leads on most Table 3 tasks (including ABIDE/NKI age, BHRC, PPMI, and ADNI), on the SALD age regression benchmark (Table 3), the voxelwise baseline SwiFT achieves better (lower) \(\text{MSE}\) (\(0.164 \pm 0.08\) vs. \(0.182 \pm 0.019\)) and higher Pearson \(\text{R}\) (\(0.926 \pm 0.003\) vs. \(0.909 \pm 0.005\)) than Omni-fMRI. A single benchmark exception does not invalidate a paper's architectural contribution—but failing to notice it when claiming "universal SOTA on all tasks" is a supervisory failure.

Read the units and denominators before the headline

1. Forensic Guide to PD01 (NARPS) Figures 1 and 2

  • Inspecting Figure 1: Look at the bars/points showing the proportion of teams reporting a significant result for Hypotheses 1–9.
    • Denominator check: What is the denominator of these fractions? It is 70 analysis teams who analyzed the same \(N = 108\) sample.
    • Epistemic trap: Why is "37.1% of teams found a significant effect for Hypothesis 1" (or "84.3% for Hypothesis 5", vs. "21.4%–37.1% across the five disputed hypotheses #1–#4 and #6") not equal to the posterior probability that the hypothesis is biologically true? Because all 70 teams share the exact same sampling noise of those 108 participants. Variance across the 70 teams measures analytical vibration (pipeline sensitivity), whereas biological truth also depends on population sampling variance (PD02) and measurement validity (PB01).
  • Inspecting Figure 2: Look at the \(64 \times 64\) correlation matrix of unthresholded whole-brain \(Z\)-maps (and its companion thresholded overlap matrix).
    • Why did the denominator change from 70 to 64? Locate the exact explanation in the text/caption (teams whose uploaded maps were missing, already thresholded, or spatially incompatible).
    • Why do continuous maps agree when binary decisions disagree? Because hard thresholding (\(Z > 3.1\) vs. cluster FWE vs. ROI mask) acts as a discontinuous step function: two teams with highly correlated unthresholded maps (\(r = 0.75\)) that sit at \(Z = 3.05\) and \(Z = 3.15\) will report opposite binary conclusions ("null" vs. "significant"). Moreover, notice the small dark band of negatively correlated maps at the edge of the matrix—caused by teams whose GLM design matrix flipped the sign or centering of the parametric gain/loss regressor.

2. Forensic Guide to PM05 (Omni-fMRI) Figure 2 and Table 3

  • Inspecting Figure 2 (Tokenization & Pretraining Contract): Compare fixed ROI parcellations and uniform grid patches against Omni-fMRI's variance-based dynamic patch tokenization (Sections 3.1–3.3). How does the tokenizer compute time-aggregated local intensity variance, discard low-intensity background, and recursively subdivide high-variance regions into finer spatial patches across multiple scales before scale-aware Masked Autoencoder (MAE) reconstruction?
  • Inspecting Table 3 (Metric Directions & The SALD Counterexample):
    • Trace the column headers in Table 3 across the age-regression datasets (ABIDE, NKI, SALD), sex classification datasets (ABCD, HCP), and clinical/phenotype tasks (BHRC, PPMI, ADNI, NKI education).
    • Mark which metrics require higher values (\(\uparrow\)) (AUROC, ACC, and Pearson correlation R) and which require lower values (\(\downarrow\)) (MSE Mean Squared Error).
    • Compare the ABIDE and NKI age-regression columns against the SALD columns. Locate one specific task where Omni-fMRI outperforms all comparators, and locate the SALD age-regression comparison in Table 3 where Omni-fMRI (MSE = 0.182 ± 0.019, R = 0.909 ± 0.005) is outperformed by the competing 4D Swin Transformer baseline SwiFT (MSE = 0.164 ± 0.08, R = 0.926 ± 0.003), while beating NeuroSTORM (0.194 / 0.892), Brain-JEPA (0.343 / 0.779), and Brain-LM (0.370 / 0.771).

Original Assignment: Rewrite the Press-Release Version

These three sweeping statements are deliberately broad caricatures invented by the course to test your ability to perform scientific claim surgery:

  1. Caricature 1 (Nihilism from NARPS): “NARPS proved that 70 neuroimaging teams analyzing the same data get completely random answers, which means fMRI is unscientific noise and can never teach us anything.”
  2. Caricature 2 (Foundation-Model Omnipotence): “The newest atlas-free fMRI foundation model is state-of-the-art on every benchmark task and operates directly on raw brain scans without needing any preprocessing.”
  3. Caricature 3 (Attention Map Reification): “Visualizing the self-attention weights of our pretrained fMRI transformer reveals the true causal neural circuit driving autism and ADHD.”

Step 1: Perform Claim Surgery

For each caricature:

  • Write a scientifically precise, source-bounded replacement (2–3 sentences) that preserves the genuine insight of PD01 or PM05 while excising the unsupported extrapolation.
  • List the specific missing evidence that would be required to justify the excised claim (e.g., what unthresholded map correlation in PD01 refutes "completely random"? What preprocessing steps in PM05 Section 4.1 / Appendix precede variance-based dynamic patching? What Table 3 row refutes "best on every task"?).

A Small Reviewer Meeting (Advocate vs. Skeptical Referee Protocol)

In top machine-learning and neuroimaging venues (ICML, NeurIPS, Nature Methods, OHBM), papers are evaluated through structured adversarial review. Conduct a 30-minute review session (either with a seminar partner or in a two-stage solo protocol):

  1. Stage 1 — The Paper's Advocate (15 minutes):
    • Present the strongest, most charitable case for PM05 (Omni-fMRI) and PD01 (NARPS). What genuine limitation of fixed ROI parcellations and uniform voxel grids does Omni-fMRI's variance-based dynamic patching solve? What empirical improvements and efficiency gains does it demonstrate in ablations (Tables 7–12, e.g., variance threshold \(\tau\) avoiding the 14k-token out-of-memory failure of uniform fine patching, multi-scale reconstruction heads, and MAE vs. JEPA objectives)?   2. Stage 2 — The Forensic Referee (15 minutes):
    • Challenge the split, baseline parity, preprocessing dependency, metric interpretation, or exception handling using exact section/table locators.
    • Rule: Do not reward an objection merely for sounding cynical ("all fMRI is noisy" or "all deep learning is a black box"). Every referee objection must point to a concrete, verifiable vulnerability in the text (e.g., Table 3 SALD age-regression underperformance relative to SwiFT, the heuristic variance-threshold tokenization limitation noted in Section 5, or the risk that non-neural physiological/motion variance could drive finer patch subdivision) and state what controlled experiment would test that objection.
  3. Stage 3 — The Meta-Reviewer Decision (200 words):
    • Write a balanced 200-word Senior Area Chair summary that synthesizes both the genuine methodological contribution and the strict boundary conditions under which the evidence holds.

Grading & Supervision Guide for R03

  1. NARPS (PD01) Denominator & Map-vs-Decision Audit:

    • Figure 1 (\(K = 70\) teams) vs. Figure 2 (\(K = 64\) teams): Ensure the student explicitly identifies that 70 teams submitted binary hypothesis decisions, while 64 teams provided usable unthresholded whole-brain statistical maps for spatial correlation analysis.
    • Why binary disagreement \(\neq\) random noise: Figure 2 proves that the underlying continuous \(Z\)-statistic maps are highly correlated across the majority of teams (and that meta-analytic consensus across pipelines is strong), while binary discrepancy is driven by thresholding choices, spatial smoothing kernels, HRF modeling assumptions, and a small subset of teams with inverted/mis-specified design-matrix regressors.

  2. Omni-fMRI (PM05, assigned arXiv v1) Forensic Audit:

    • The Table 3 SALD Exception: Require every student to locate the SALD age-regression columns in Table 3, identify that MSE (\(\downarrow\)) is lower-is-better while Pearson R (\(\uparrow\)) is higher-is-better, and observe that under full fine-tuning Omni-fMRI (MSE = 0.182 ± 0.019, R = 0.909 ± 0.005) is outperformed by the competing 4D Swin Transformer baseline SwiFT (MSE = 0.164 ± 0.08, R = 0.926 ± 0.003), even though Omni-fMRI beats SwiFT under frozen linear probing in Table 6 (0.354 vs. 0.440 MSE) and leads on most other Table 3 benchmarks. This is a classic lesson in reading tables closely rather than relying solely on abstract headlines.
    • "Atlas-Free" \(\neq\) "Preprocessing-Free": Emphasize that Omni-fMRI's inputs are preprocessed 4D fMRI volumes resampled to \(2\text{ mm}\) isotropic resolution, cropped/padded to \(96^3\), temporally interpolated to \(\text{TR} = 0.72\text{ s}\), and globally \(z\)-scored. "Atlas-free" means replacing a fixed anatomical ROI parcellation (like Schaefer or AAL) with variance-based dynamic spatial patches—which, as Section 5 notes, still relies on a heuristic variance threshold.
    • Attribution Maps \(\neq\) Causal Mechanisms: While Section 4.5 aligns Integrated Gradients saliency maps with Neurosynth functional terms, predictive attribution indicates statistical utility for the downstream classifier, not a verified causal neural mechanism (PB01).

Agentic Deliverable Supervision (R03 · macOS Goose + Ollama: Qwen & Gemma)

Deliverable focus: Paired audit of multi-team analytical variability (PD01 NARPS) and patch-based fMRI foundation modeling (PM05 Omni-fMRI).

1. Suggestive Prompt (in the spirit of what to ask — adapt, do not copy verbatim)

Ask Goose/Gemma in the spirit of: "Using my excerpts from NARPS (PD01 Table 1 & Figures 1–2) and Omni-fMRI (PM05 Figures 1–2), help me contrast how pipeline degrees of freedom across 70 analysis teams affect binary hypothesis decisions versus how a patch-based fMRI foundation model defines its input tokens, masks, and benchmark baselines."

2. What to Look For & How to Trace the Error Back Down

  • Immediate Red Flag: The AI treats the 70 NARPS team results as 70 independent datasets (ignoring that all 70 teams analyzed the exact same \(N = 108\) participants) or assumes that replacing a traditional parcel atlas with an end-to-end Transformer (PM05) eliminates preprocessing and thresholding sensitivity.
  • Where the Bug Entered: Check the denominator in PD01 (\(70\) teams \(\times 9\) hypotheses on \(1\) shared dataset of \(N = 108\) subjects) and check the input preprocessing and baseline comparators in PM05.
  • How to Fix It: Enforce explicit coordinate, shape, and degrees-of-freedom assertions before accepting the output.

3. Expected Outcome Range & Why It Must Look That Way

Expected Range: In PD01, across the 9 tested hypotheses, binary team agreement splits sharply—from near-consensus (\(\sim 84\%\) positive on Hypothesis 5) to wide disagreement (\(\sim 20\text{–}37\%\) positive on Hypotheses 1–4, 6) despite high spatial map correlations (\(r \approx 0.5\text{–}0.8\)). Why: Continuous unthresholded \(z\)-maps share a dominant underlying signal mode, but binary significance thresholding (\(p < 0.05\) FWE/FDR) applies a discontinuous step function right near the decision boundary, amplifying small smoothing, design, and contrast differences into opposite binary conclusions.
