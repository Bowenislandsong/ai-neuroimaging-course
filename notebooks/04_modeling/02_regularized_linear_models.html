Governing Mathematical Formulation & Term-by-Term Logic

Elastic Net Regularized Objective (Unifying Ridge L2 and Lasso L1)

Balances data fidelity against an L1 sparsity penalty (alpha=1) and an L2 Tikhonov grouping penalty (alpha=0) that adds lambda to every eigenvalue of X^T X.

\[\hat{\mathbf{w}} = \arg\min_{\mathbf{w}} \left\{ \underbrace{\frac{1}{2N}\| \mathbf{y} - X\mathbf{w} \|_2^2}_{\text{Empirical Data Fit}} + \lambda \Big( \underbrace{\alpha \|\mathbf{w}\|_1}_{\text{Lasso Sparsity}} + \underbrace{\frac{1-\alpha}{2}\|\mathbf{w}\|_2^2}_{\text{Ridge Grouping}} \Big) \right\}, \qquad \hat{\mathbf{w}}_{\text{Ridge}} = (X^T X + \lambda I_p)^{-1} X^T \mathbf{y}\]
Logical Term Role in Equation What This Part Is Doing (Mechanism & Units) What Breaks If Omitted or Mis-Specified (Failure Mode)
\((X^T X + \lambda I_p)^{-1}\)Ridge Eigenvalue StabilizationShifts every singular value sigma_j^2 of X^T X upward to sigma_j^2 + lambda, making inversion well-conditioned even when P >> N.Fitting regularized models without standardizing feature scales first, which penalizes small-unit features unfairly.
\(\lambda \alpha \|\mathbf{w}\|_1\)L1 Diamond Corner Sparsity PenaltyDrives weak weights to exact 0 via soft-thresholding, but exhibits unstable selection when brain voxels are strongly correlated.Claiming the 95% of voxels zeroed by Lasso are 'not involved in the task' when they were merely collinear with the surviving 5%.

Why this technique matters

High-dimensional neuroimaging regressions—from voxelwise encoding (himalaya, Nilearn) to connectome-wide prediction and linear probing of foundation models (BrainIAC)—routinely operate in the p >> N regime with spatially correlated voxels. Without appropriate L2 or Elastic Net regularization, ordinary least squares suffers severe variance inflation along near-collinear dipole directions, while pure L1 (Lasso) arbitrarily drops correlated partner voxels across bootstrap resamples (Zou & Hastie, 2005). Understanding penalty geometry and singular-value shrinkage forces principled choices between dense distributed shrinkage (Ridge/banded Ridge) and grouped sparse selection (Elastic Net) inside fold-safe scaling pipelines.

Key references · Regularized linear models (Ridge L2, Lasso L1, Elastic Net, and regularized logistic probing) under collinearity

  • Foundational — Hoerl and Kennard (1970), Ridge Regression: Biased Estimation for Nonorthogonal Problems, Technometrics. Introduces ridge regression and proves that adding a positive diagonal penalty trades a small bias for a large reduction in estimator variance under collinearity.
  • Foundational — Tibshirani (1996), Regression Shrinkage and Selection Via the Lasso, Journal of the Royal Statistical Society Series B: Statistical Methodology. Introduces the L1-penalized Lasso estimator for simultaneous continuous shrinkage and exact variable selection.
  • Foundational — Zou and Hastie (2005), Regularization and Variable Selection Via the Elastic Net, Journal of the Royal Statistical Society Series B: Statistical Methodology. Combines L1 and L2 penalties in the Elastic Net to preserve sparsity while grouping correlated predictors instead of arbitrarily dropping all but one.
  • State of the art — Dupré la Tour et al. (2022), Feature-space selection with banded ridge regression, NeuroImage. Demonstrates banded ridge regression for high-dimensional voxelwise encoding models with multiple correlated feature spaces.

1. Foundational & SOTA Paper Motivation: Regularization Is a Structural Prior

In high-dimensional neuroimaging, the number of spatial measurements \(P\) (thousands of cortical voxels, surface vertices, or connectome edges) routinely approaches or exceeds the number of independent observations \(N\) (trials or participants). Even when \(N > P\), neighboring voxels are strongly correlated due to spatial smoothness of the point-spread function, shared vascular drainage, and coherent functional organization. Under collinearity or \(P \approx N\), Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) becomes ill-posed: the sample covariance matrix \(\frac{1}{N} X^\top X\) has near-zero eigenvalues \(\sigma_i^2 \to 0\), causing the unregularized inverse \((X^\top X)^{-1}\) to amplify measurement noise by \(1/\sigma_i^2\).

Every regularized linear model resolves this ill-posedness by committing to an explicit structural prior over how neural information is distributed across features:

  • Naselaris et al. (2011; PM01) and Scotti et al. (2024; MindEye2, PM06) rely on Ridge regression (\(L_2\) regularization) for voxel-wise encoding and fMRI-to-latent decoding. Why \(L_2\) rather than sparse \(L_1\) (Lasso) for visual cortex decoding? Because natural visual representations are distributed across correlated retinotopic neighborhoods. When two neighboring voxels measure the same underlying cortical column (\(x_1 \approx x_2\)), Ridge shares weight symmetrically between them (\(\hat{w}_1 \approx \hat{w}_2\)), stabilizing out-of-sample predictions and producing reproducible spatial maps.
  • Conversely, when the scientific hypothesis posits that only a small subset of features carries the true effect amidst hundreds of pure-noise distractors (e.g., selecting a compact panel of regional structural biomarkers), Lasso (\(L_1\) regularization) enforces exact sparsity (\(\hat{w}_j = 0\)) via soft-thresholding. However, under strong voxel collinearity, the \(L_1\) penalty suffers from selection instability: an infinitesimal perturbation of the training fold causes Lasso to arbitrarily drop one correlated voxel and assign the entire weight to its neighbor.
  • Finally, in Tak et al. (2026; BrainIAC, PM03), evaluating pretrained 3D MRI foundation models via linear probing fits an \(L_2\)-regularized Logistic Regression head on frozen latent features to predict binary or multi-class clinical endpoints, mapping unbounded linear log-odds into calibrated class probabilities on \([0, 1]\) rather than regressing binary \(\{0, 1\}\) labels with squared error.

2. Mathematical Formulation & Spectral Mechanics

2.1 SVD Spectral Shrinkage: OLS vs. Ridge (\(L_2\))

Let \(X \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times P}\) be the standardized design matrix with Singular Value Decomposition (SVD):

\[X = U \Sigma V^\top = \sum_{i=1}^{r} \sigma_i u_i v_i^\top\]

where \(U \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times r}\) and \(V \in \mathbb{R}^{P \times r}\) have orthonormal columns (\(U^\top U = V^\top V = I_r\)) and \(\sigma_1 \ge \sigma_2 \ge \dots \ge \sigma_r > 0\) are the singular values. The Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) estimator projects \(y \in \mathbb{R}^N\) onto the principal directions \(v_i\) with filter factors \(f_i^{\text{OLS}} = 1\):

When two voxels are strongly correlated (\(\rho \to 1\)), their antisymmetric difference mode \(v_- = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(e_1 - e_2)\) has tiny singular value \(\sigma_- = \sqrt{N(1 - \rho)} \to 0\). OLS divides noise by \(\sigma_- \approx 0\), causing \(\text{Var}(\hat{w}_{\text{OLS}})\) to explode.

Ridge Regression (\(L_2\)) adds a quadratic penalty \(\alpha \|w\|_2^2\) (\(\alpha > 0\)):

\[\hat{w}_{\text{Ridge}}(\alpha) = \arg\min_{w \in \mathbb{R}^P} \| y - X w \|_2^2 + \alpha \| w \|_2^2 = (X^\top X + \alpha I_P)^{-1} X^\top y = \sum_{i=1}^{r} \underbrace{\left( \frac{\sigma_i^2}{\sigma_i^2 + \alpha} \right)}_{f_i^{\text{Ridge}}(\alpha)} \frac{1}{\sigma_i} (u_i^\top y) v_i\]

The spectral shrinkage factor \(f_i^{\text{Ridge}}(\alpha) = \frac{\sigma_i^2}{\sigma_i^2 + \alpha} \in (0, 1)\) smoothly damps low-variance collinear directions (\(\sigma_i^2 \ll \alpha\)) while preserving high-variance signal directions (\(\sigma_i^2 \gg \alpha\)). Moreover, because the \(L_2\) ball \(\{w : w_1^2 + w_2^2 \le c\}\) is strictly convex and rotationally symmetric, identical correlated features \(x_1 = x_2\) are guaranteed to receive identical weights \(\hat{w}_1 = \hat{w}_2\).

2.2 Lasso (\(L_1\)) Soft-Thresholding & Collinear Instability

Lasso replaces the smooth quadratic penalty with the non-differentiable \(L_1\) norm:

\[\hat{w}_{\text{Lasso}}(\lambda) = \arg\min_{w \in \mathbb{R}^P} \frac{1}{2N} \| y - X w \|_2^2 + \lambda \| w \|_1, \qquad \| w \|_1 = \sum_{j=1}^{P} |w_j|\]

For an orthonormal design (\(X^\top X = N I_P\)), the Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) subgradient optimality condition \(0 \in -\frac{1}{N} X^\top (y - X w) + \lambda \partial \|w\|_1\) yields the closed-form soft-thresholding operator \(S_\lambda(z_j)\):

\[\hat{w}_{\text{Lasso}, j} = \text{sign}(z_j) \max(|z_j| - \lambda, 0), \qquad z_j = \frac{1}{N} x_j^\top y\]

Any feature whose marginal residual correlation \(|z_j|\) falls below \(\lambda\) is set to exact zero. However, when two features are nearly collinear (\(x_1 \approx x_2\)), the level sets of the loss are parallel to the flat face \(w_1 + w_2 = c\) of the \(L_1\) diamond \(|w_1| + |w_2| \le c\). Consequently, Lasso's solution across bootstrap resamples hops erratically between \((c, 0)\) and \((0, c)\). Elastic Net combines \(\lambda_1 \|w\|_1 + \lambda_2 \|w\|_2^2\) to retain sparsity while rounding the \(L_1\) corners so correlated voxel groups are selected together.

2.3 \(L_2\)-Regularized Logistic Regression for Binary Targets

When the target is binary \(c_n \in \{0, 1\}\) (e.g., responder vs. non-responder in BrainIAC PM03), linear regression produces unbounded predictions \(\hat{y} \in (-\infty, \infty)\) and violates homoscedastic Gaussian noise assumptions. Logistic regression models the posterior log-odds (logit) linearly via the sigmoid function \(\sigma(z) = (1 + e^{-z})^{-1}\):

\[P(c_n = 1 \mid x_n) = \sigma(w^\top x_n + b) = \frac{1}{1 + \exp(-(w^\top x_n + b))} \in (0, 1)\]

5. Regularization Path, SVD Spectral Shrinkage & Collinear Bootstrap Stability

To make the geometry of regularization concrete, we run two quantitative experiments in Code Cell 3:

  1. Full Regularization Path Sweep (\(\alpha \in [10^{-3}, 10^4]\)): We evaluate held-out test MSE and coefficient trajectories across 25 log-spaced penalty strengths for both Ridge and Lasso, identifying the bias-variance sweet spot where variance reduction outweighs shrinkage bias.

6. Quantitative Interpretation & Your Investigation

Interpreting the 3-Panel Diagnostic Dashboard

  • Panel (A) — The U-Shaped Bias-Variance Trajectory: Unregularized OLS (MSE = 1.98) overfits the \(64\) pure-noise columns and the low-variance difference mode \(x_0 - x_1\). As \(\alpha\) increases along the Ridge path, variance drops rapidly until \(\alpha \approx 12\) (MSE = 0.87), before rising sharply toward the null variance (MSE = 11.40) when \(\alpha = 10^5\) crushes the signal modes.
  • Panel (B) — Sparse vs. Distributed Recovery: Because \(64\) out of \(70\) features are pure Gaussian noise (\(w_j^* = 0\) for \(j \ge 6\)), Lasso (MSE = 0.50) outperforms Ridge (MSE = 0.87) in overall prediction error by setting \(59\) noise coefficients to exact zero. Yet look at the gold-shaded region (Features 0 and 1, where \(w_0^* = w_1^* = 1.50\)): Ridge recovers symmetric weights [+1.49, +1.49], whereas Lasso assigns +2.45 to Feature 0 and +0.51 to Feature 1, and OLS swings wildly to [-1.53, +4.47].
  • Panel (C) — Bootstrap Instability Under Collinearity: Across \(40\) bootstrap resamples of the training cohort, the twin weight difference \(\hat{w}_0 - \hat{w}_1\) stays tightly concentrated near zero for Ridge (\(\text{SD} \approx 0.16\)) but flips wildly between \(-3.0\) and \(+3.0\) for Lasso (\(\text{SD} > 1.8\)). This proves why \(L_1\) feature selection cannot be interpreted as a unique causal discovery of "which voxel matters" when features are correlated.

Exit questions and answer key

  1. Why do both Ridge and Lasso improve held-out MSE over OLS here, yet produce radically different weight vectors? Both reduce estimation variance on the \(64\) noise columns and the collinear twin pair, but Ridge (\(L_2\)) shrinks all coordinates continuously and shares weight symmetrically across correlated features, whereas Lasso (\(L_1\)) soft-thresholds small coordinates to exact zero and picks an arbitrary corner on correlated twins.

  2. If Lasso sets Feature 1's weight near zero while giving Feature 0 a large weight, does that prove Feature 1 is biologically uninformative? No. When two features are strongly correlated (\(x_0 \approx x_1\)), the \(L_1\) norm \(|w_0| + |w_1|\) is constant along \(w_0 + w_1 = c\), so Lasso arbitrarily assigns the weight to whichever feature had a slightly higher sample correlation with \(y\) in that specific training split.

  3. Why is LogisticRegression mathematically preferable to Ridge regression when predicting a binary clinical diagnosis \(y \in \{0, 1\}\)? Logistic regression maps linear log-odds through the sigmoid \(\sigma(z) \in (0, 1)\) and optimizes the Bernoulli log-likelihood, guaranteeing valid probability bounds and heteroscedastic variance \(p(1-p)\), whereas linear regression produces unbounded predictions outside \([0, 1]\).

  4. Why must StandardScaler() always be placed inside make_pipeline before Ridge or Lasso? Because \(L_1\) and \(L_2\) penalties are not scale-invariant (they penalize raw coefficient units), and fitting the scaler inside the pipeline prevents test-set mean and variance from leaking into training standardization.
