Representational Similarity Analysis (RSA), Cross-Validated Mahalanobis (Crossnobis) Distance & Noise Ceilings

Question: How can two representations be compared when their individual feature dimensions do not correspond?

Record a prediction, a source location, and one point you want this lesson to clarify. Ask your AI tutor to distinguish the paper’s evidence from its interpretation.

Why this technique matters

Representational Similarity Analysis (Kriegeskorte et al., 2008; Nili et al., 2014; rsatoolbox) enables quantitative comparison between brain regions, species, and deep neural network layers by abstracting from voxel coordinates to second-order pairwise condition distances. However, uncrossvalidated squared Euclidean or Mahalanobis distances suffer a strictly positive quadratic noise bias that converts heteroscedastic trial noise (e.g., high-motion or low-trial conditions) into illusory representational clusters (Walther et al., 2016). Using cross-validated distance estimators across independent imaging runs and permuting condition labels jointly across rows and columns are essential for unbiased geometry inference (Schütt et al., 2023).

Key references · Representational Similarity Analysis (RSA), Representational Dissimilarity Matrices (RDMs), cross-validated distances (crossnobis), and condition-label permutation inference

  • Standard tool — Nili et al. (2014), A Toolbox for Representational Similarity Analysis, PLoS Computational Biology. Introduces the Representational Similarity Analysis (RSA) toolbox, condition-label permutation testing, and Kendall's tau-a rank correlation for models with tied dissimilarities.
  • State of the art — Schütt et al. (2023), Statistical inference on representational geometries, eLife. Establishes rsatoolbox 3.0's cross-validated bootstrap and two-factor (subject-by-stimulus) statistical inference framework for comparing representational geometries.
  • Critical evaluation — Walther et al. (2016), Reliability of dissimilarity measures for multi-voxel pattern analysis, NeuroImage. Proves that uncrossvalidated distance estimators suffer positive noise bias and demonstrates that cross-validated Mahalanobis (crossnobis) distances restore unbiased zero-centered geometry.
  • Review — Diedrichsen and Kriegeskorte (2017), Representational models: A common framework for understanding encoding, pattern-component, and representational-similarity analysis, PLOS Computational Biology. Unifies encoding models, pattern component modeling, and second-order RSA matrices under a common second-moment representational geometry framework.

1. Foundational & SOTA Paper Motivation: Comparing Geometry Across Brains and AI Models

How can we test whether the ventral visual stream of Human Participant A, macaque inferior temporal (IT) single-unit recordings, and Layer 18 of a Vision Transformer represent visual categories in the same way when no individual voxel, neuron, or artificial unit corresponds one-to-one across systems? In their foundational paper, Kriegeskorte, Mur, and Bandettini (2008; PM02), Representational similarity analysis – connecting the branches of systems neuroscience, solved this coordinate-mismatch problem by moving from first-order activation space (voxels/units) to second-order representational geometry. For a fixed set of \(C\) experimental stimulus conditions, each system yields a symmetric \(C \times C\) Representational Dissimilarity Matrix (RDM) whose entry \((i, j)\) quantifies how differently conditions \(i\) and \(j\) are represented within that system. Two systems with completely different sensor dimensions (\(V_1 = 4{,}000\) fMRI voxels vs. \(V_2 = 4096\) MindEye2 latent dimensions in Scotti et al., 2024, PM06) can then be compared directly by computing the rank correlation (Spearman's \(\rho\) or Kendall's \(\tau_A\)) between the upper triangles of their RDMs.

However, contemporary RSA practice (Walther et al., 2016, NeuroImage; Diedrichsen & Kriegeskorte, 2017) emphasizes a critical statistical flaw in naive within-run Euclidean RDMs: squared Euclidean distances are positively biased by measurement noise. Even when two experimental conditions evoke the exact same true neural pattern (\(\beta_i^* = \beta_j^*\), so true distance \(d_{ij}^* = 0\)), independent noise fluctuations \(\varepsilon_i, \varepsilon_j\) ensure that \(\|\hat{\beta}_i - \hat{\beta}_j\|_2^2 > 0\) with positive expectation \(2 \text{tr}(\Sigma_E)\). If noise variance differs across brain regions or conditions, naive RDMs confound noise amplitude with representational geometry. The rigorous modern solution is the Cross-Validated Squared Euclidean / Mahalanobis (Crossnobis) Estimator, which takes the inner product of pattern differences across independent scanning runs A and B.

2. Mathematical Formulation & Statistical Proof of Unbiasedness

2.1 Representational Dissimilarity Matrix (RDM) & Upper-Triangular Indexing

Let \(B = [\hat{\beta}_1, \dots, \hat{\beta}_C]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times V}\) denote the estimated response patterns across \(C\) ordered stimulus conditions and \(V\) measurement channels. The pairwise dissimilarity matrix \(D \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times C}\) with Euclidean metric is:

\[D_{ij} = \| \hat{\beta}_i - \hat{\beta}_j \|_2 = \sqrt{\sum_{v=1}^{V} (\hat{\beta}_{iv} - \hat{\beta}_{jv})^2}\]

\(\sqrt{\sum_{v=1}^{V} (\hat{\beta}_{iv} - \hat{\beta}_{jv})^2}\)Weighted Aggregation / Marginalization TermSums or integrates contributions across discrete states, spatial neighbors, basis functions, or time points.

Because \(D\) is symmetric (\(D_{ij} = D_{ji}\)) with a zero diagonal (\(D_{ii} = 0\)), only the \(M = \binom{C}{2} = \frac{C(C-1)}{2}\) strictly upper-triangular entries (np.triu_indices(C, k=1)) carry unique relational information. Including the diagonal or both triangles when correlating two RDMs \(D^{(1)}\) and \(D^{(2)}\) artificially inflates the correlation and invalidates degrees of freedom.

2.2 Theorem: Positive Noise Bias of Ordinary Squared Euclidean Distance

Suppose in a single scanning run \(A\), the estimated condition pattern is \(\hat{\beta}_i^{(A)} = \beta_i^* + \varepsilon_i^{(A)}\), where \(\beta_i^* \in \mathbb{R}^V\) is the true deterministic neural pattern and \(\varepsilon_i^{(A)} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \Sigma_E)\) is zero-mean spatial noise independent across conditions. Define the true difference \(\Delta_{ij}^* = \beta_i^* - \beta_j^*\) and the noisy difference \(\hat{\Delta}_{ij}^{(A)} = \hat{\beta}_i^{(A)} - \hat{\beta}_j^{(A)} = \Delta_{ij}^* + (\varepsilon_i^{(A)} - \varepsilon_j^{(A)})\). What is the expected value of the ordinary within-run squared Euclidean distance?

\[\mathbb{E}\left[ \| \hat{\Delta}_{ij}^{(A)} \|_2^2 \right] = \mathbb{E}\left[ \left( \Delta_{ij}^* + \varepsilon_i^{(A)} - \varepsilon_j^{(A)} \right)^\top \left( \Delta_{ij}^* + \varepsilon_i^{(A)} - \varepsilon_j^{(A)} \right) \right]\]

Because \(\mathbb{E}[\varepsilon_i^{(A)}] = \mathbb{E}[\varepsilon_j^{(A)}] = 0\) and \(\varepsilon_i^{(A)} \perp \varepsilon_j^{(A)}\), the cross terms vanish and the noise variances add:

\[\mathbb{E}\left[ \| \hat{\Delta}_{ij}^{(A)} \|_2^2 \right] = \| \Delta_{ij}^* \|_2^2 + \mathbb{E}[\|\varepsilon_i^{(A)}\|_2^2] + \mathbb{E}[\|\varepsilon_j^{(A)}\|_2^2] = \| \Delta_{ij}^* \|_2^2 + 2 \, \text{tr}(\Sigma_E) > \| \Delta_{ij}^* \|_2^2\]

When \(V = 20\) channels each have unit noise variance \(\sigma^2 = 1\) (\(\text{tr}(\Sigma_E) = 20\)) and the true distance is zero (\(\|\Delta_{ij}^*\|_2^2 = 0\)), the ordinary squared distance has an expected value of \(2 \times 20 = +40.0\).

2.3 Theorem: Zero-Bias Cross-Validated Distance (Crossnobis Estimator)

Now suppose we estimate the condition patterns independently in two separate scanning partitions (Runs \(A\) and \(B\)), where \(\varepsilon^{(A)} \perp \varepsilon^{(B)}\) (\(\mathbb{E}[\varepsilon_i^{(A)} \varepsilon_k^{(B)\top}] = 0\)). The cross-validated squared Euclidean distance (and its noise-whitened Crossnobis generalization using \(\hat{\Sigma}_E^{-1}\)) replaces the squared norm with the cross-run inner product:

\[\hat{d}_{\text{cv}}^2(i, j) = \left( \hat{\beta}_i^{(A)} - \hat{\beta}_j^{(A)} \right)^\top \left( \hat{\beta}_i^{(B)} - \hat{\beta}_j^{(B)} \right), \qquad \hat{d}_{\text{Crossnobis}}^2(i, j) = \left( \hat{\Delta}_{ij}^{(A)} \right)^\top \hat{\Sigma}_E^{-1} \left( \hat{\Delta}_{ij}^{(B)} \right)\]

\(\mathbb{E}[\cdot], \; \mathbb{P}(\cdot)\)Expectation / Probability MeasureEvaluates the population expectation or conditional probability law over the specified random variables and conditioning set.

The positive \(2\,\text{tr}(\Sigma_E)\) noise bias vanishes identically. Notice an essential mathematical consequence: while a true squared distance \(\|\Delta_{ij}^*\|_2^2\) is non-negative, individual sample estimates \(\hat{d}_{\text{cv}}^2(i, j)\) can be negative when true distance is near zero, which is necessary for the sample mean across null pairs to be unbiased around \(0.0\).

2.4 Valid Condition-Label Permutation Inference

Entries of an RDM share row and column conditions: \(D_{12}\) and \(D_{13}\) both depend on condition \(1\). Therefore, the \(\binom{C}{2}\) upper-triangular entries are not statistically independent, and permuting the \(\binom{C}{2}\) vector entries directly violates matrix structure. Valid RSA permutation testing permutes the \(C\) condition labels simultaneously across both rows and columns: \(D_{\text{perm}} = D[\pi, :][:, \pi]\).

4. Deliberate Methodological Failure Modes: Within-Run Distance Bias & Reused-Run Leakage

Predict the three behaviors before running the next cell:

  1. Positive Noise Bias of Ordinary Squared Distance (ordinary): Across \(5{,}000\) Monte Carlo simulations where Conditions \(0\) and \(1\) are pure standard normal noise (\(\beta_1^* = \beta_0^* = 0\), \(V = 20\) channels), the ordinary squared distance \(\|\hat{\beta}_1^{(A)} - \hat{\beta}_0^{(A)}\|_2^2\) averages \(\approx 40.0\) instead of \(0.0\).
  2. Reused-Run Leakage (leaked = np.sum(da * da, axis=1)): If a pipeline bug accidentally passes Run \(A\) into both arguments of a cross-validated distance function (or splits correlated trials from the same run), the noise terms correlate (\(\varepsilon^{(A)} = \varepsilon^{(B)}\)) and the \(+40.0\) positive bias returns in full (np.array_equal(ordinary, leaked)).

5. Exact Condition-Permutation Null Distribution & Heteroscedastic Noise Bias Audit

Why is the unbiased cross-validated distance essential when comparing representations (PM02, PM06)? Suppose Condition Pairs 0..2 are measured during high-arousal or high-motion blocks (higher noise variance \(\sigma_{\text{high}}^2 = 1.8^2\)) while Condition Pairs 3..5 have low noise variance (\(\sigma_{\text{low}}^2 = 0.3^2\)), even though no neural category signal exists anywhere. In Code Cell 3, we demonstrate:

  1. Heteroscedastic Noise Creates a Fake Category RDM in Ordinary Euclidean Distance: Ordinary within-run Euclidean distance mistakes the higher noise variance of Conditions 0..2 for large neural representational distances, producing a spurious correlation with a block model RDM. Cross-validated distance (Crossnobis) completely eliminates this heteroscedastic variance artifact.
  2. Exact \(6! = 720\) Condition-Label Permutation Test: We enumerate all \(720\) simultaneous row/column permutations \(\pi \in S_6\) of the \(6 \times 6\) RDM to compute the exact non-parametric \(p\)-value of our category RSA match.
Python · Executable Cell 3
import itertools

# 1. Enumerate all 6! = 720 valid simultaneous row/column condition permutations
all_perms = list(itertools.permutations(range(6)))
null_spearman = np.empty(len(all_perms))
for idx_p, p_tuple in enumerate(all_perms):
    p_arr = np.array(p_tuple)
    m_perm = model[np.ix_(p_arr, p_arr)]
    null_spearman[idx_p] = spearmanr(rdm[upper], m_perm[upper]).statistic

exact_p_val = float(np.mean(null_spearman >= r - 1e-12))
print(f"Observed Spearman r = {r:.4f} | Exact permutation p-value across 6! = 720 permutations: p = {exact_p_val:.4f}")
assert exact_p_val <= 0.12 and len(all_perms) == 720

# 2. Heteroscedastic noise across conditions fools ordinary RDM but NOT cross-validated RDM
rng_het = np.random.default_rng(4080)
cond_noise_sd = np.array([0.2, 0.2, 0.2, 1.5, 1.5, 1.5])[:, None]  # Conditions 3..5 are noisier, ZERO true signal
het_run_a = rng_het.normal(size=(200, 6, 20)) * cond_noise_sd[None, :, :]
het_run_b = rng_het.normal(size=(200, 6, 20)) * cond_noise_sd[None, :, :]

rdm_ord_het = np.zeros((6, 6))
rdm_cv_het = np.zeros((6, 6))
for i in range(6):
    for j in range(6):
        diff_a = het_run_a[:, i, :] - het_run_a[:, j, :]
        diff_b = het_run_b[:, i, :] - het_run_b[:, j, :]
        rdm_ord_het[i, j] = np.mean(np.sum(diff_a * diff_a, axis=1))
        rdm_cv_het[i, j] = np.mean(np.sum(diff_a * diff_b, axis=1))

print("Heteroscedastic null RDM off-diagonal range — Ordinary:", round(float( np.ptp(rdm_ord_het[upper]) ), 2),
      "vs Cross-validated:", round(float( np.ptp(rdm_cv_het[upper]) ), 2))
assert np.ptp(rdm_ord_het[upper]) > 35.0 and np.ptp(rdm_cv_het[upper]) < 3.0

Interpreting the 3-Panel Diagnostic Dashboard

  • Panel (A) — Coordinate-Free Geometry Comparison (PM02): Even though the empirical patterns live in a 12-voxel measurement space and the categorical model is defined symbolically, their \(6 \times 6\) RDMs share the exact same \(3 \times 3\) block-diagonal topology (Spearman \(\rho = 0.85\) across the \(15\) unique upper-triangular pairs).

Exit questions and answer key

  1. Why must only the strictly upper-triangular entries (np.triu_indices(C, 1)) of an RDM be passed to spearmanr? Because an RDM is symmetric (\(D_{ij} = D_{ji}\)) with structural zeros on the diagonal (\(D_{ii} = 0\)); including the diagonal mixes zero self-distances with between-condition distances, and including both triangles duplicates every pair.

  2. Why can a cross-validated squared distance \(\hat{d}_{\text{cv}}^2(i, j)\) be negative when true squared distance is non-negative? Because \(\hat{d}_{\text{cv}}^2 = (\Delta^* + \varepsilon^{(A)})^\top (\Delta^* + \varepsilon^{(B)})\) is an inner product of two noisy difference vectors; when \(\Delta^* \approx 0\), random anti-alignment of \(\varepsilon^{(A)}\) and \(\varepsilon^{(B)}\) produces negative values so the mean is unbiased at \(0\).

  3. Why does valid RSA permutation testing permute model[np.ix_(perm, perm)] rather than shuffling the 15 upper-triangular numbers independently? Matrix entries sharing a row or column condition are statistically dependent; only simultaneous row-and-column permutations preserve the metric graph structure of the RDM.

  4. How does RSA allow comparing a human visual cortex ROI with a layer of MindEye2 (PM06) when they have different numbers of channels? Both systems are summarized by their \(C \times C\) condition-by-condition dissimilarity matrices, abstracting away voxel/unit coordinates while preserving second-order representational geometry (PM02).

2. What to Look For & How to Trace the Error Back Down

  • Immediate Red Flag: Under pure null noise, an ordinary squared Euclidean RDM has a large positive off-diagonal mean (40.2156) and a 92.2-unit false range across conditions with different trial counts/noise levels, or reusing the same run for both halves of crossnobis reproduces the +40.22 positive bias.
  • Where the Bug Entered: For noisy pattern estimates \(\hat{u}_i = u_i + \epsilon_i\), \(\mathbb{E}[\|\hat{u}_i - \hat{u}_j\|_2^2] = \|u_i - u_j\|_2^2 + \text{tr}(\Sigma_i) + \text{tr}(\Sigma_j) > 0\); if conditions have unequal trial counts (heteroscedastic \(\Sigma_i\)), the noise trace creates a structured false RDM.
  • How to Fix It: Compute the cross-validated inner product \((\hat{u}_i^{(A)} - \hat{u}_j^{(A)})^\top (\hat{u}_i^{(B)} - \hat{u}_j^{(B)})\) across strictly independent runs \(A \ne B\), extract only np.triu_indices(6, k=1), and permute condition labels synchronously across rows and columns.

3. Expected Outcome Range & Why It Must Look That Way

Why: Independent run errors satisfy \(\mathbb{E}[(\epsilon_i^{(A)} - \epsilon_j^{(A)})^\top (\epsilon_i^{(B)} - \epsilon_j^{(B)})] = 0\) even when \(\Sigma_i \ne \Sigma_j\).
