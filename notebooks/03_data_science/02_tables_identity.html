Record a prediction, a source location, and one point you want this lesson to clarify. Ask your AI tutor to distinguish the paper’s evidence from its interpretation.

Why this technique matters

Neuroimaging datasets in BIDS format store demographics at the participant level, clinical visits at the session level, and QC metrics at the scan-run level. Careless inner/many-to-many joins silently drop participants or duplicate rows, and splitting at the scan-run level (KFold) rather than the participant level (GroupKFold) leaks individual connectome fingerprints across train and test folds, producing severely inflated predictive R^2.

Key references · Tidy relational tables, BIDS multi-level key joins, and participant-grouped cross-validation

  • Foundational — Wickham (2014), Tidy Data, Journal of Statistical Software. Formalizes tidy relational data principles (one observation per row, one variable per column, one observational unit per table) required to separate participant, session, and run tables.
  • Standard tool — Gorgolewski et al. (2016), The brain imaging data structure, a format for organizing and describing outputs of neuroimaging experiments, Scientific Data. Specifies the Brain Imaging Data Structure (BIDS) relational hierarchy linking participants.tsv, sessions, and run-level imaging metadata via composite keys.
  • State of the art — Kapoor and Narayanan (2023), Leakage and the reproducibility crisis in machine-learning-based science, Patterns. Surveys data leakage taxonomies across scientific machine learning, highlighting non-independence across repeated measurements and premature preprocessing joins.
  • Critical evaluation — Varoquaux et al. (2017), Assessing and tuning brain decoders: Cross-validation, caveats, and guidelines, NeuroImage. Demonstrates why cross-validation in neuroimaging must group repeated runs and sessions at the participant level to avoid connectome-fingerprint data leakage.

Understand the transformation

1. SOTA & Foundational Paper Motivation: Why Table Joins Decide Cohort Integrity

In large-scale human neuroimaging, a participant's scientific record is never stored in a single flat spreadsheet. Demographic intake tables, clinical diagnostic batteries, scanner acquisition logs, fMRIPrep quality-control (confounds_timeseries.tsv / desc-confounds_timeseries.json), and regional morphometry tables (aparc / aseg cortical thickness and subcortical volumes) live in separate relational tables linked by composite keys (participant_id, session_id, run_id, site_id). Two landmark papers assigned to this lesson show why relational identity and join cardinality are central scientific concerns rather than mundane clerical details:

  • Marek et al. (2022), Reproducible brain-wide association studies require thousands of individuals (PD02, Main Text & Methods on ABCD / UK Biobank cohort construction, Figures 1 and 3): Brain-Wide Association Studies (BWAS) estimate small phenotypic correlations (\(|r| \approx 0.05\text{–}0.15\)) across thousands of participants in multi-session consortia such as ABCD (\(N > 11,000\)) and UK Biobank (\(N > 35,000\)). When participants have multiple longitudinal sessions (ses-baselineYear1Arm1, ses-2YearFollowUpYArm1), repeated resting-state runs (run-01, run-02), or twin/sibling family structures (rel_family_id), joining a participant-level behavioral table (\(1\) row per participant_id) against a scan-level QC or morphometry table (\(m\) rows per participant_id) without explicit cardinality rules silently multiplies rows. Worse, if two multi-row tables are merged on participant_id alone (ignoring session_id), a \(1:m \times 1:k \to m \times k\) Cartesian explosion fabricates cross-session hybrid rows that never existed in reality, inflating nominal sample size \(N\) and leaking identical participants across cross-validation splits.
  • Fortin et al. (2018), Harmonization of cortical thickness measurements across scanners and sites (PD07, Introduction & Methods Section 2.3 on ComBat): Multi-site harmonization models additive (\(\gamma_{ig}\)) and multiplicative (\(\delta_{ig}\)) scanner batch effects while preserving biological covariates \(X_j \beta_g\) (age, sex, clinical diagnosis). ComBat requires an exact \(1:1\) alignment between rows of the cortical thickness feature matrix \(Y \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times V}\), the site vector \(b \in \{1,\dots,M\}^N\), and the biological design matrix \(X \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times p}\). If inconsistent subject identifiers (sub-01 vs 01 vs sub_01), trailing whitespace, or silent inner-join drops remove high-severity patients from one site, site effects become confounded with biological severity before ComBat even runs.

2. University Curriculum Alignment

This lesson connects UC Berkeley Data 8 (Chapter 8.4: Joining Tables by Columns) and Data 100 (Relational Algebra, Primary/Foreign Keys, and Tidy Data / Hadley Wickham's 3NF) with BIDS (Brain Imaging Data Structure) metadata contracts taught in Graduate Neuroimaging Data Science and Graduate fMRI Analysis. In tidy neuroimaging tables, every table must declare its observational grain (participant-level, session-level, run-level, or ROI-level) and its primary key tuple before any merge operation is permitted.

3. Mathematical Formulation: Relational Keys, Join Cardinality, and Variance Distortion

Let a relational table \(T\) be a finite set of tuples over attributes \(\mathcal{A}(T)\), equipped with a candidate key \(K \subseteq \mathcal{A}(T)\).

A. Primary Key Uniqueness Invariant

A subset of columns \(K\) is a valid primary key for table \(T\) if and only if the projection \(\pi_K(T)\) has no missing values (NULL/NaN) and its cardinality equals the row count of \(T\):

\[\forall r \in T,\; \pi_K(r) \neq \varnothing \qquad \text{and} \qquad \left|\pi_K(T)\right| = |T|\]

In BIDS neuroimaging cohorts, the canonical primary keys at each observational hierarchy are:

  1. Participant table (participants.tsv): \(K_{\text{subj}} = (\texttt{participant\_id})\)
  2. Session table (*sessions.tsv): \(K_{\text{ses}} = (\texttt{participant\_id}, \texttt{session\_id})\)
  3. Run / Scan QC table (bold_qc.tsv): \(K_{\text{run}} = (\texttt{participant\_id}, \texttt{session\_id}, \texttt{run\_id})\)

B. Equi-Join Cardinality Algebra (\(1:1\), \(1:m\), and \(m:n\) Explosions)

Consider an equi-join \(T_{\text{join}} = A \bowtie_K B\) on shared join columns \(K\). For each distinct key value \(k \in \pi_K(A) \cup \pi_K(B)\), let \(m_A(k) = |\{a \in A : \pi_K(a) = k\}|\) and \(m_B(k) = |\{b \in B : \pi_K(b) = k\}|\) denote the multiplicity of key \(k\) in tables \(A\) and \(B\). The total number of rows produced by an inner join is:

\[\left|A \bowtie_K^{\text{inner}} B\right| = \sum_{k \in \pi_K(A) \cap \pi_K(B)} m_A(k) \cdot m_B(k)\]

  • \(1:1\) Join (validate="one_to_one"): \(m_A(k) \le 1\) and \(m_B(k) \le 1\) for all \(k\). Row count is bounded by \(\min(|A|, |B|)\).
  • \(1:m\) Join (validate="one_to_many"): \(m_A(k) \le 1\) while \(m_B(k) \ge 1\). Each participant's demographic row in \(A\) is replicated \(m_B(k)\) times across their scans in \(B\). If the analyst subsequently computes a "participant-level" mean or standard error across all rows of \(T_{\text{join}}\) without re-aggregating to participant_id, participant \(k\)'s weight is inflated by \(m_B(k)\):

\[\bar{y}_{\text{naive}} = \frac{\sum_{k=1}^{N} m_B(k)\, y_k}{\sum_{k=1}^{N} m_B(k)} \neq \frac{1}{N} \sum_{k=1}^{N} y_k = \bar{y}_{\text{participant}}\]

Part 1 · Core Mathematical Implementation & BIDS Multi-Site Cohort Tables

We construct a realistic synthetic multi-site BIDS neuroimaging cohort inspired by Marek et al. (PD02) and Fortin et al. (PD07) with \(N=60\) unique participants across 3 sites (SiteA, SiteB, SiteC), 2 longitudinal sessions (ses-01, ses-02), and variable run counts (1 to 3 runs per session, where higher-motion clinical participants require extra repeat runs). We also include realistic ID formatting inconsistencies (sub-001 vs SUB-001 vs 001) that must be canonicalized before joining.

import numpy as np
import pandas as pd

rng = np.random.default_rng(202)
n_participants = 60

# 1. Participant-level demographic & clinical table (1 row per participant)
subj_ids_clean = [f"sub-{i:03d}" for i in range(1, n_participants + 1)]
sites = np.repeat(['SiteA', 'SiteB', 'SiteC'], n_participants // 3)
# SiteC recruits older clinical patients; also has messy ID formatting in raw exports
ages = np.concatenate([
    rng.normal(25.0, 4.0, 20),
    rng.normal(40.0, 6.0, 20),
    rng.normal(68.0, 5.5, 20),
]).round(1)
symptom_score = (0.35 * (ages - 40.0) + rng.normal(0.0, 4.0, n_participants)).round(2)

raw_demo_ids = []
for idx, (sid, site) in enumerate(zip(subj_ids_clean, sites)):
    if site == 'SiteC' and idx % 2 == 0:
        raw_demo_ids.append(sid.upper() + " ")  # e.g., "SUB-041 "
    elif site == 'SiteB' and idx % 5 == 0:
        raw_demo_ids.append(sid.replace("sub-", ""))  # e.g., "021"
    else:
        raw_demo_ids.append(sid)

demo_raw_df = pd.DataFrame({
    'participant_id': raw_demo_ids,
    'site_id': sites,
    'age_years': ages,
    'symptom_score': symptom_score,
})

# 2. Session-level cognitive battery table (2 sessions per participant = 120 rows)
session_rows = []
for i, sid in enumerate(subj_ids_clean):
    for ses_idx, ses in enumerate(['ses-01', 'ses-02']):
        mem_score = 100.0 - 0.30 * (ages[i] - 30.0) - 1.2 * ses_idx + rng.normal(0.0, 3.5)
        session_rows.append({
            'participant_id': sid,
            'session_id': ses,
            'memory_score': round(float(mem_score), 2),
        })
sessions_df = pd.DataFrame(session_rows)

# 3. Run-level fMRIPrep / morphometry QC table (1 to 3 runs per session; older/symptomatic subjects get repeat scans)
scan_rows = []
for i, sid in enumerate(subj_ids_clean):
    n_runs = 3 if symptom_score[i] > 5.0 else (2 if symptom_score[i] > -1.0 else 1)
    for ses in ['ses-01', 'ses-02']:
        for r_idx in range(1, n_runs + 1):
            fd_mm = max(0.05, 0.12 + 0.004 * (ages[i] - 25.0) + rng.normal(0.0, 0.04))
            thickness_mm = 2.65 - 0.006 * (ages[i] - 25.0) - 0.08 * (sites[i] == 'SiteC') + rng.normal(0.0, 0.05)
            scan_rows.append({
                'participant_id': sid,
                'session_id': ses,
                'run_id': f"run-0{r_idx}",
                'mean_fd_mm': round(float(fd_mm), 4),
                'frontal_thickness_mm': round(float(thickness_mm), 4),
                'hippocampal_vol_mm3': round(float(3800.0 - 14.0 * (ages[i] - 25.0) + rng.normal(0.0, 120.0)), 1),
            })
scans_df = pd.DataFrame(scan_rows)

def canonicalize_bids_id(series: pd.Series) -> pd.Series:
    cleaned = series.astype(str).str.strip().str.lower()
    numeric_part = cleaned.str.replace(r'^sub[-_]?', '', regex=True)
    return numeric_part.apply(lambda s: f"sub-{int(s):03d}")

demo_clean_df = demo_raw_df.copy()
demo_clean_df['participant_id'] = canonicalize_bids_id(demo_clean_df['participant_id'])

assert demo_clean_df['participant_id'].is_unique
assert len(demo_clean_df) == n_participants
assert not sessions_df.duplicated(subset=['participant_id', 'session_id']).any()
assert not scans_df.duplicated(subset=['participant_id', 'session_id', 'run_id']).any()

print(f"Participants table: {len(demo_clean_df)} rows (unique IDs: {demo_clean_df['participant_id'].nunique()})")
print(f"Sessions table:     {len(sessions_df)} rows (grain: participant_id × session_id)")
print(f"Scans/Runs table:   {len(scans_df)} rows (grain: participant_id × session_id × run_id)")

We now demonstrate three classic table-joining disasters that corrupt multi-site neuroimaging analyses (PD02, PD07):

  1. Unstandardized Key Inner-Join Drop: Merging demo_raw_df with sessions_df via how='inner' silently drops half of SiteC (the oldest, most symptomatic clinical cohort) and 20% of SiteB because of 'SUB-041 ' and '021' string mismatches.
  2. Many-to-Many (\(m:n\)) Cross-Session Cartesian Explosion: Merging sessions_df (120 rows) and scans_df (224 rows) on on='participant_id' alone (omitting session_id) creates \(2 \times \text{runs} = 456\) rows—fabricating chimeric rows where ses-01 memory scores are paired with ses-02 cortical thickness.
  3. Pseudoreplication Bias in Participant Means: Computing cohort symptom scores across scan-level rows without first aggregating to participant_id over-weights high-symptom patients who underwent 3 repeat scans per session.

Part 3 · Tidy Restructuring (melt / pivot) & Cross-Validation Split Leakage Audit

We next demonstrate how to convert wide regional morphometry columns (frontal_thickness_mm, hippocampal_vol_mm3) into a tidy long format with explicit metric_unit metadata, and we run a quantitative simulation of row-level KFold vs subject-grouped GroupKFold on the merged scan table. When a participant has multiple runs/sessions in full_scan_table, a naive row-level train/test split places run-01 in the training set and run-02 of the exact same brain in the test set—inflating out-of-sample \(R^2\) via subject-identity memorization (PD02).

from sklearn.model_selection import KFold, GroupKFold
from sklearn.metrics import r2_score

# 1. Aggregate run-level scans to session-level and subject-level summaries cleanly
subject_summary_df = (
    full_scan_table
    .groupby(['participant_id', 'site_id'], as_index=False)
    .agg(
        age_years=('age_years', 'first'),
        symptom_score=('symptom_score', 'first'),
        n_total_scans=('run_id', 'count'),
        mean_fd_mm=('mean_fd_mm', 'mean'),
        frontal_thickness_mm=('frontal_thickness_mm', 'mean'),
        hippocampal_vol_mm3=('hippocampal_vol_mm3', 'mean'),
    )
)
assert len(subject_summary_df) == n_participants
assert subject_summary_df['participant_id'].is_unique

# 2. Tidy long format with explicit physical units (never mix mm and mm^3 in one unlabelled value column!)
unit_lookup = {'frontal_thickness_mm': 'mm', 'hippocampal_vol_mm3': 'mm^3'}
tidy_morph_df = subject_summary_df.melt(
    id_vars=['participant_id', 'site_id', 'age_years'],
    value_vars=['frontal_thickness_mm', 'hippocampal_vol_mm3'],
    var_name='morphometry_feature',
    value_name='measured_value',
)
tidy_morph_df['physical_unit'] = tidy_morph_df['morphometry_feature'].map(unit_lookup)
assert set(tidy_morph_df['physical_unit']) == {'mm', 'mm^3'}

# 3. Quantify subject identity leakage when splitting scan rows (KFold) vs participants (GroupKFold)
# Create subject-specific random anatomical fingerprints (P=15 features) shared across repeats of the same person
subj_fingerprints = {sid: rng.normal(0.0, 1.0, 15) for sid in subj_ids_clean}
X_scans = np.array([
    subj_fingerprints[r.participant_id] + rng.normal(0.0, 0.08, 15)
    for _, r in full_scan_table.iterrows()
])
# Target y is a subject-specific random trait correlated with their fingerprint in the sample via high ridge flexibility
subj_trait = {sid: float(subj_fingerprints[sid][:5].sum() + rng.normal(0.0, 3.0)) for sid in subj_ids_clean}
y_scans = np.array([subj_trait[r.participant_id] for _, r in full_scan_table.iterrows()])
groups_scans = full_scan_table['participant_id'].to_numpy()

from sklearn.neighbors import KNeighborsRegressor
kf_row = KFold(n_splits=5, shuffle=True, random_state=42)
gkf_subj = GroupKFold(n_splits=5)

r2_row_leaked, overlap_counts = [], []
for tr_idx, te_idx in kf_row.split(X_scans, y_scans):
    overlap = len(set(groups_scans[tr_idx]) & set(groups_scans[te_idx]))
    overlap_counts.append(overlap)
    model = KNeighborsRegressor(n_neighbors=5).fit(X_scans[tr_idx], y_scans[tr_idx])
    r2_row_leaked.append(r2_score(y_scans[te_idx], model.predict(X_scans[te_idx])))

r2_group_clean = []
for tr_idx, te_idx in gkf_subj.split(X_scans, y_scans, groups=groups_scans):
    assert len(set(groups_scans[tr_idx]) & set(groups_scans[te_idx])) == 0
    model = KNeighborsRegressor(n_neighbors=5).fit(X_scans[tr_idx], y_scans[tr_idx])
    r2_group_clean.append(r2_score(y_scans[te_idx], model.predict(X_scans[te_idx])))

print(f"Row-level KFold on joined scan table: mean subject overlap per fold = {np.mean(overlap_counts):.1f}/{len(set(groups_scans))} subjects!")
print(f"Row-level KFold (Leaked Subject Identity) Mean R^2:   {np.mean(r2_row_leaked):+.3f}")
print(f"Subject-level GroupKFold (Zero Overlap) Mean R^2:     {np.mean(r2_group_clean):+.3f}")
assert np.mean(r2_row_leaked) > 0.50
assert np.mean(r2_group_clean) < 0.25

Python · Executable Cell 5
def verify_relational_join_contract(
    left_df: pd.DataFrame,
    right_df: pd.DataFrame,
    on_keys: list[str],
    expected_cardinality: str = 'one_to_many',
) -> dict:
    # Audit primary/foreign key integrity and join cardinality before downstream modeling.
    left_dups = int(left_df.duplicated(subset=on_keys).sum())
    right_dups = int(right_df.duplicated(subset=on_keys).sum())
    left_keys = set(map(tuple, left_df[on_keys].to_numpy()))
    right_keys = set(map(tuple, right_df[on_keys].to_numpy()))
    unmatched_left = len(left_keys - right_keys)
    unmatched_right = len(right_keys - left_keys)
    merged = pd.merge(left_df, right_df, on=on_keys, how='inner', validate=expected_cardinality)
    return {
        'join_keys': '+'.join(on_keys),
        'cardinality': expected_cardinality,
        'left_rows': len(left_df),
        'right_rows': len(right_df),
        'merged_rows': len(merged),
        'left_dups': left_dups,
        'right_dups': right_dups,
        'unmatched_left_keys': unmatched_left,
        'unmatched_right_keys': unmatched_right,
    }

audit_records = [

  • Immediate Red Flag: A naive pd.merge(..., how='inner') silently drops the cohort from N = 60 to N = 46 participants (losing 50.0% of older clinical SiteC and shifting mean age from 45.18 to 40.58 years), or row-level KFold on the 224-scan table reports \(R^2 = +0.591\) on a target with zero between-subject signal.
  • Where the Bug Entered: Inconsistent string padding (sub-001 vs. sub-1) causes silent inner-join row deletion concentrated in one hospital site, and row-level KFold places repeated sessions/runs from 31.8 / 60 subjects in both train and test folds simultaneously.
  • How to Fix It: Canonicalize IDs before joining, pass validate='one_to_many' and check indicator=True for unmatched keys, and split strictly with GroupKFold(n_splits=5).split(X, y, groups=df['participant_id']).

3. Expected Outcome Range & Why It Must Look That Way

Expected Range: In DS02, canonicalizing IDs retains all 60 participants (120 sessions, 224 scans) with 0 unmatched keys and preserves the true mean cohort age (45.18 yrs vs. 40.58 yrs under the buggy inner join); eliminating the 31.8-subject fold overlap via GroupKFold drops the leaked row-wise \(R^2\) from +0.591 to -0.456. Why: Repeated scans from the same participant share high trait/anatomical ICC, so row-level splits memorize participant identity rather than predicting unseen individuals.
