Governing Mathematical Formulation & Term-by-Term Logic

Single-Run Task fMRI General Linear Model & Residual Autocorrelation Diagnostic

Models each voxel's BOLD time series as a sum of HRF-convolved task predictors and discrete cosine drift terms, then audits temporal autocorrelation in the residuals.

\[\mathbf{y}_v = \underbrace{X_{\text{task}}\boldsymbol{\beta}_{\text{task}, v}}_{\text{HRF-Convolved Auditory}} + \underbrace{X_{\text{drift}}\boldsymbol{\beta}_{\text{drift}, v}}_{\text{DCT Low-Freq Drift}} + \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_v, \qquad \hat{\rho}_1(v) = \frac{\sum_{t=2}^{T} \hat{\varepsilon}_{v,t}\hat{\varepsilon}_{v,t-1}}{\sum_{t=1}^{T}\hat{\varepsilon}_{v,t}^2}\]
Logical Term Role in Equation What This Part Is Doing (Mechanism & Units) What Breaks If Omitted or Mis-Specified (Failure Mode)
\(X_{\text{task}} = (s_{\text{auditory}} * h)\big|_{t_n}\)Scan-Sampled Hemodynamic PredictorAuditory block boxcar convolved with canonical double-gamma HRF and sampled at the 84 retained TRs (TR=7.0 s).Misaligning event onsets when initial dummy scans were already discarded.
\(X_{\text{drift}}\)Discrete Cosine High-Pass BasisModels slow scanner baseline drift jointly inside the GLM design matrix.Omitting drift terms, allowing slow baseline ramps to inflate residual variance sigma-hat^2.
\(\hat{\rho}_1(v)\)Lag-One Residual Autocorrelation MapQuantifies serial correlation remaining in OLS residuals to determine whether GLS prewhitening is required.Claiming single-subject OLS t-values represent population-level inference.

Why this technique matters

First-level GLM fitting translates a 4D functional MRI time series and stimulus event log into subject-level effect-size and statistical maps that feed every downstream group analysis or decoding study. Without verifying BIDS sidecar timing metadata, cosine drift cutoff frequencies, and residual autocorrelation, an analyst can silently desynchronize stimulus onsets from brain volumes, absorb the task regressor into high-pass drift columns (inflating variance by ~7x when the cutoff encroaches on the task frequency), or misstate single-session statistical precision. Auditing the GLM forces explicit decisions about dummy-scan provenance, high-pass cutoff relative to task period, prewhitening noise models, and reporting unthresholded effect and uncertainty maps alongside FDR-thresholded displays.

Key references · First-level mass-univariate task-fMRI General Linear Model (BIDS time-axis alignment, HRF convolution, DCT drift filtering, AR(1) prewhitening, and voxelwise FDR inference)

  • Foundational — Friston et al. (1994), Statistical parametric maps in functional imaging: A general linear approach, Human Brain Mapping. Establishes the mass-univariate General Linear Model and statistical parametric mapping framework for functional neuroimaging.
  • Foundational — Woolrich et al. (2001), Temporal Autocorrelation in Univariate Linear Modeling of FMRI Data, NeuroImage. Demonstrates why temporal autocorrelation in fMRI time series biases OLS standard errors and formalizes prewhitening for valid first-level GLS inference.
  • Foundational — Genovese et al. (2002), Thresholding of Statistical Maps in Functional Neuroimaging Using the False Discovery Rate, NeuroImage. Introduces Benjamini-Hochberg False Discovery Rate (FDR) thresholding for controlling expected false discovery proportions across neuroimaging voxel families.
  • Critical evaluation — Olszowy et al. (2019), Accurate autocorrelation modeling substantially improves fMRI reliability, Nature Communications. Evaluates autocorrelation modeling across major fMRI software packages and shows how prewhitening accuracy depends on TR, experimental design, and residual structure.

2. Why BIDS Sidecar Provenance Controls the Time Axis (PP01)

A generic AI assistant asked to "write an fMRI preprocessing script" may insert a boilerplate line like func_img = func_img.slicer[..., 5:] or func_img.slicer[..., 12:] to "discard initial T1 equilibration dummy scans." Doing that blindly on this dataset corrupts the experimental timeline.

  • Inspecting the BIDS JSON sidecar (task-auditory_bold.json, Gorgolewski et al., 2016) reveals:
    • Total delivered volumes: \(T = 84\) scans (LAS voxel array orientation, \(3 \times 3 \times 3\text{ mm}^3\));
    • Repetition Time: \(\text{TR} = 7.0\text{ seconds}\) (total delivered duration \(= 84 \times 7.0 = 588\text{ s}\));
    • "NumberOfVolumesDiscardedByUser": 12 — 12 volumes (\(84\text{ s}\), one complete rest+listening cycle) were already discarded before the file was packaged, and the event table (events.tsv, listing seven \(42\text{ s}\) binaural bi-syllabic word listening blocks at onset = 42, 126, ..., 546 s, with the interleaved \(42\text{ s}\) rest blocks starting at \(t = 0\text{ s}\) serving as the implicit baseline) is already synchronized to Volume 0 of the delivered 84-volume file.
  • If an unsupervised script drops 5 or 6 volumes (\(35\text{--}42\text{ s}\), roughly half an \(84\text{ s}\) cycle) without shifting events.tsv, the periodic regressor shifts by \(\approx 150^\circ\text{--}180^\circ\) and inverts task and rest (flipping the auditory cortex \(z\)-peak negative). Even more insidiously, dropping 12 volumes (\(84\text{ s}\)) shifts the timeline by one full \(84\text{ s}\) cycle, keeping the periodic regressor in phase so the activation map still looks plausible while silently discarding a full cycle and misaligning the end of the event table. Input metadata—never generic AI habits—must govern time-axis alignment.

3. Mathematical Specification of the First-Level Mass-Univariate GLM

At each inside-brain voxel \(v \in \{1, \dots, V\}\), the observed time series \(\mathbf{y}_v \in \mathbb{R}^{T}\) (\(T = 84\)) is modeled as a linear combination of \(P\) design-matrix predictors \(\mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times P}\) plus serially correlated noise \(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_v\):

\[\mathbf{y}_v = \mathbf{X}\boldsymbol{\beta}_v + \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_v, \qquad \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_v \sim \mathcal{N}\!\left(\mathbf{0},\, \sigma_v^2 \mathbf{V}_v\right)\]

Equation TermLogical RoleWhat This Part Is Doing
\(\mathbf{y}_v\)Target Quantity (LHS · Exact Equality)The output quantity, transformed signal, or statistical estimator being defined or solved for on the left-hand side.
\(\mathbf{X}\boldsymbol{\beta}_v\)Additive Component 1 of 2Contributes additively to the composite mathematical expression on the right-hand side.
\(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_v\)Variance / Noise / Error ContributionCaptures measurement noise variance, biological variability, or residual uncertainty that propagates into the total signal dispersion.
\(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}_v \sim \mathcal{N}\!\left(\mathbf{0},\, \sigma_v^2 \mathbf{V}_v\right)\)Coupled Constraint / Implied IdentityDefines the coupled consequence, auxiliary parameter, or boundary condition paired with the primary equation.
\(\mathcal{N}(\mu, \Sigma)\)Gaussian Sampling / Posterior DistributionSpecifies the normal probability law parameterized by its mean vector (first argument) and variance/covariance structure (second argument).

  1. The Design Matrix \(\mathbf{X} = [\mathbf{x}_{\text{task}},\, \mathbf{D}_{\text{drift}},\, \mathbf{1}]\):
    • Hemodynamic Task Predictor (\(\mathbf{x}_{\text{task}}\)): The boxcar stimulus function \(s(t) \in \{0, 1\}\) (\(42\text{ s}\) ON, \(42\text{ s}\) OFF; fundamental period \(T_{\text{cycle}} = 84\text{ s}\), fundamental frequency \(f_{\text{task}} = \frac{1}{84\text{ s}} \approx 0.0119\text{ Hz}\)) is convolved in high resolution with the SPM canonical double-gamma HRF \(h(t)\) (PB01, F02) and sampled at the \(\text{TR} = 7.0\text{ s}\) grid:

\[x_{\text{task}}[k] = (s * h)(k \cdot \text{TR}), \quad k = 0, 1, \dots, 83\]
∑ Equation Logic Breakdown · Which part of this equation is doing what (4 logical terms)
Equation TermLogical RoleWhat This Part Is Doing
\(x_{\text{task}}[k]\)Target Quantity (LHS · Exact Equality)The output quantity, transformed signal, or statistical estimator being defined or solved for on the left-hand side.

- **Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) Drift Basis (\(\mathbf{D}_{\text{drift}}\)):** Low-frequency scanner thermal drift and physiological aliasing are modeled using cosine basis functions \(d_m[n] = \sqrt{\frac{2}{T}}\cos\!\left(\frac{\pi (2n + 1) m}{2T}\right)\) for scan index \(n \in \{0, \dots, T-1\}\) with frequency \(f_m = \frac{m}{2 T \cdot \text{TR}}\) up to high-pass cutoff \(f_c = 0.01\text{ Hz}\) (period \(100\text{ s}\), yielding \(M = \lfloor 2 T \cdot \text{TR} \cdot f_c \rfloor = 11\) cosines with \(f_{11} \approx 0.0094\text{ Hz}\); cf. SPM's default \(128\text{ s} \approx 0.0078\text{ Hz}\)).
 - *Critical Frequency Interaction:* Because the task cycle period is \(84\text{ s}\) (\(f_{\text{task}} = \frac{1}{84\text{ s}} \approx 0.0119\text{ Hz}\)), a high-pass cutoff of \(f_c = 0.010\text{ Hz}\) stays just below \(f_{\text{task}}\) (\(\text{VIF} = 1.14\)). Increasing high_pass to \(0.015\text{ Hz}\) (\(M = 17\) cosines) introduces the \(m = 14\) cosine at \(f_{14} = \frac{14}{2 \times 84 \times 7\text{ s}} = \frac{1}{84}\text{ Hz} \approx 0.0119\text{ Hz}\)—directly on top of \(f_{\text{task}}\), inflating the centered \(\text{VIF}(\hat{\beta}_{\text{listening}})\) from \(1.14\) to \(7.95\) (\(6.96\times\)) and absorbing the auditory task signal into the nuisance drift subspace.
- **Signal Scaling (signal_scaling=0, standardize=False):** Scales each voxel's demeaned time series by \(100 / \bar{y}_v\) so that \(\hat{\beta}_v\) is expressed in **Percent Signal Change (PSC)** relative to local temporal mean (F01, F03).
  1. AR(1) Prewhitening & Generalized Least Squares (GLS): Typical short-TR fMRI noise exhibits positive temporal autocorrelation (\(\varepsilon_{v,t} = \rho_v \varepsilon_{v,t-1} + u_{v,t}\)). As established by Purdon & Weisskoff (1998), Woolrich et al. (2001), and Olszowy et al. (2019) (whereas Eklund et al., 2016, PD05 showed how non-Gaussian spatial autocorrelation inflates cluster-extent FWE rates), ignoring positive temporal autocorrelation in Ordinary Least Squares (\(\mathbf{V} = \mathbf{I}\)) underestimates \(\text{Var}(\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_v)\) and inflates false positives. Under an \(\text{AR}(1)\) model \(V_{t,t'} = \rho_v^{|t - t'|}\) (estimated per voxel from OLS residuals via Yule–Walker lag-1 autocorrelation quantized into \(0.01\) bins in Nilearn), prewhitening multiplies \(\mathbf{y}_v\) and \(\mathbf{X}\) by \(\mathbf{W}_v = \mathbf{V}_v^{-1/2}\), yielding the Generalized Least Squares estimator (Friston et al., 1994; Woolrich et al., 2001):

\[\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{v,\text{GLS}} = \left(\mathbf{X}^\top \mathbf{V}_v^{-1} \mathbf{X}\right)^{-1} \mathbf{X}^\top \mathbf{V}_v^{-1} \mathbf{y}_v\]

Step 2 · Fit and Inspect the First-Level Design Matrix (\(\mathbf{X}\)) & Audit High-Pass Cutoff Collinearity

def task_vif(df_mat):
    M = df_mat.to_numpy()
    C = np.linalg.inv(M.T @ M)
    x0 = M[:, 0] - M[:, 0].mean()
    return float(C[0, 0] * np.sum(x0 ** 2))

Step 3 · Distinguish Contrast Effect Size (\(\hat{\beta}\)), Uncertainty (\(z\)-Score), and FDR Thresholded Display

Compute the unstandardized/PSC contrast effect_size (\(\mathbf{c}^\top \hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_v\)), its standard error (\(\sqrt{\text{Var}(\mathbf{c}^\top \hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_v)}\)), and the standardized z_score (\(z_v\)), and apply two-sided voxelwise Benjamini-Hochberg FDR control at \(\alpha = 0.05\) over the fitted brain mask (\(V_{\text{mask}}\) voxels) with cluster_threshold=0. Step 4 · Forensic Residual & Sensitivity Dashboard: Lag-1 Autocorrelation and Effect-vs-\(Z\) Dissociation

A fitted statistical map is never the end of a GLM audit. Extract the voxelwise model residuals \(\mathbf{e}_v = [e_{v,1}, \dots, e_{v,T}]\) across all \(V_{\text{mask}}\) inside-brain voxels, compute the sample lag-1 serial correlation \(\hat{r}_{1,v}\), and build a 3-panel diagnostic dashboard comparing:

  1. Panel A (Raw Residual Lag-1 Autocorrelation Distribution): Inspects the unwhitened raw residuals \(\mathbf{y}_v - \mathbf{X}\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_v\) returned by model.residuals[0]. Unlike short-TR (\(\le 2\text{ s}\)) fMRI where physiological noise induces strong positive lag-1 autocorrelation (Purdon & Weisskoff, 1998; Olszowy et al., 2019), at \(\text{TR} = 7.0\text{ s}\) after projecting out a constant plus 11 low-frequency DCT drift terms the empirical lag-1 residual correlation is centered slightly below zero (\(\text{median } \hat{r}_1 \approx -0.050\), with \(\approx 31\%\) of voxels positive) due to the known downward finite-sample bias \(\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf{R}\mathbf{L})/\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf{R}) < 0\) of detrended OLS/GLS residuals.
  2. Panel B (Contrast Effect Size \(\hat{\beta}_v\) vs. \(z\)-Statistic \(z_v\)): Demonstrates that although effect size and \(z\)-score correlate strongly (\(\rho_{\text{Spearman}} \approx 0.96\)), roughly one-fifth of the top \(1\%\) largest-PSC voxels still fall below the two-sided FDR threshold (\(|z| = 2.55\)) because their local standard error \(\text{SE}_v\) is large (e.g., near draining veins or CSF boundaries).
  3. Panel C (Task Predictor Time Course After Drift Projection): Visualizes the unique variance of the listening predictor after projecting out the cosine drift subspace under high_pass = 0.010 Hz vs. the colliding high_pass = 0.015 Hz specification (where the unique sum of squares drops by \(6.96\times\), matching the VIF inflation from \(1.14\) to \(7.95\)).

🎓 Answer guide

  1. BIDS Sidecar & Time Synchronization: The delivered 84-volume NIfTI series already excludes 12 initial volumes (NumberOfVolumesDiscardedByUser = 12). In events.tsv, the seven \(42\text{ s}\) listening blocks start at onset = 42, 126, ..., 546 s (with the alternating \(42\text{ s}\) rest blocks starting at \(t = 0\text{ s}\) as the implicit baseline). Dropping 6 volumes (\(42\text{ s}\)) without shifting onsets shifts the design by half a cycle and flips the sign of the auditory activation, whereas dropping 12 volumes (\(84\text{ s}\), one full cycle) keeps the periodic regressor in phase while silently losing a cycle of data.
  2. Scaling, Thresholding, & Noise-Model Sensitivity: signal_scaling=0 divides demeaned time series by voxel baseline mean (\(\times 100\)) so \(\hat{\beta}\) is in Percent Signal Change; standardize=False does not disable signal_scaling=0. Two-sided voxelwise FDR (\(\alpha = 0.05\), Genovese et al., 2002) bounds the expected false discovery proportion among rejected voxels in this run (\(|z| \ge 2.55\), retaining \(13,260\) of \(61,335\) mask voxels under ar1). Because lag-1 residual correlation at \(\text{TR} = 7.0\text{ s}\) is weak and slightly negative on average (\(\text{median } \hat{r}_1 \approx -0.050\)), switching noise_model from 'ar1' to 'ols' yields a very similar map (max |z| = 9.70 vs. 9.40; 11,459 vs. 13,260 retained voxels), whereas at short TRs (\(\le 2\text{ s}\)) positive autocorrelation makes OLS anti-conservative (Purdon & Weisskoff, 1998; Woolrich et al., 2001; Olszowy et al., 2019).
  3. Drift Cutoff Collision: At high_pass = 0.015 Hz (\(17\) cosine columns), the 14th cosine has frequency \(14/(2 \times 84 \times 7\text{ s}) = 1/84\text{ Hz} \approx 0.0119\text{ Hz} = f_{\text{task}}\), yielding \(R^2 \approx 0.874\) when regressing listening on the nuisance columns, inflating the centered VIF from \(1.14\) to \(7.95\) (\(6.96\times\)) and absorbing true task variance into the drift subspace.
  4. Missing Preprocessing & \(N=1\) Limit: Because raw functional EPI (LAS, \(3\text{ mm}\) isotropic) and anatomical \(T_1\text{w}\) (256×256×54, \(1\times 1\times 3\text{ mm}^3\)) scans lack motion realignment (MCFLIRT), slice-timing correction, susceptibility distortion unwarping (no fieldmap is provided in MoAE), and boundary-based coregistration (PP01), overlaying thresholded on data.anat at scanner world coordinates cut_coords=[0, 20, 40] relies solely on raw header affines. 2. What to Look For & How to Trace the Error Back Down

  • Immediate Red Flag: Calling model.compute_contrast('listening', output_type='variance') raises a ValueError in Nilearn 0.14+, or raising the high-pass DCT cutoff from 0.01 Hz (11 drifts) to 0.015 Hz (14 drifts) causes the task VIF to jump from 1.14 to 7.95 (6.96x variance inflation) and erases auditory cortex \(z\)-scores.
  • Where the Bug Entered: The valid Nilearn contrast key is 'effect_variance'; and in this block design (6 volumes rest + 6 volumes listening = 12 volumes \(\times 7.0\text{ s} = 84\text{ s}\) period), the fundamental task frequency is \(f_{\text{task}} = 1 / 84\text{ s} = 0.0119\text{ Hz}\), which lies below 0.015 Hz (drift_14 has frequency \(14 / (2 \times 588\text{ s}) = 0.0119\text{ Hz}\), collinear with listening).
  • How to Fix It: Always verify that the high-pass cutoff \(f_{\text{hp}} < f_{\text{task}}\) (0.01 Hz < 0.0119 Hz), compute centered task VIF \(C_{00}\sum_t (x_t - \bar{x})^2\), and pass output_type='effect_variance'.

3. Expected Outcome Range & Why It Must Look That Way

Expected Range: In P02 (curriculum/real_fmri_run.json), across 84 scans (TR = 7.0 s) and 61,335 brain-mask voxels, the 13-column GLM (0.01 Hz high-pass, task VIF = 1.14) yields max |z| = 9.40 and a two-sided BH-FDR (\(q = 0.05\)) threshold of |z| >= 2.55 retaining 13,260 suprathreshold voxels, whereas the buggy 0.015 Hz cutoff inflates task VIF to 7.95 (6.96x).
